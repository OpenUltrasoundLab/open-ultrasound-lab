From echo to photon: the ultrasound display chain

Every B-mode pixel a clinician sees has passed through a dozen engineering decisions after the beamformer: compression, gray map, scan conversion, persistence, 8 or 10 bits, a monitor's tone curve and the light in the room. Treat that chain as a system you design and verify, and you can say with numbers whether a lesion the beamformer resolved is still visible on the screen.

You will be able to

  • Budget bits and decibels through the chain, from the ADC to the just-noticeable differences (JNDs) of the monitor in its room
  • Design log or μ-law compression, dynamic range, gain and gray maps, and say which image-quality metrics they change (CNR) and which they can only lower (gCNR)
  • Compute the DICOM Grayscale Standard Display Function (GSDF), the JND count a display realizes, and a calibration lookup table
  • Design scan conversion (line density, interpolation domain, zoom) and a persistence filter whose time constant survives a frame-rate change
  • Choose colour maps and colour/gray arbitration, calibrate measurements (pixel spacing, DICOM ultrasound regions, sound-speed error) and store images without undoing the display design
  • Use the display QA documents (AAPM TG18 and Report 270, IEC 62563-1, the ACR–AAPM–SIIM technical standard, DICOM PS3.14) without quoting limits you have not checked

In 60 seconds
  1. Bit budget: The display maps echo level in dB onto gray levels spread over the displayed dynamic range (DR). One gray step is \(\Delta_{\mathrm{dB}} = \mathrm{DR}/(2^{b_o}-1)\), with \(b_o\) the output bit depth: 0.235 dB for 60 dB in 8 bits, tiny against the 5.57 dB spread of log speckle. Clipping, monitor and room lose more than bits. §34.1
  2. Compression and gray maps: Time-gain compensation lifts noise with tissue, so aim for a noise floor just below the displayed range at the deepest depth of interest. Any one-to-one pointwise gray map leaves the gCNR (one minus the lesion–background histogram overlap) of the underlying distributions unchanged; many-to-one steps such as clipping, reject or coarse quantization can lower it, and finite-sample histogram estimates vary with bins. Adaptive gain breaks the gray-to-echo mapping, so measure on IQ data. §34.2
  3. Detectability: A lesion's visibility depends on how many just-noticeable differences (JNDs) its contrast spans on screen: display JND slope × gray-map slope × contrast over dynamic range. With a linear 60 dB map on a calibrated 615-JND display, a −6 dB lesion spans about 61.5 JND, similar to the speckle texture, so speckle usually limits detection. §34.3
  4. DICOM GSDF: The DICOM Grayscale Standard Display Function (GSDF) makes equal steps of stored gray value look equally different. It redistributes just-noticeable differences but cannot create them; their total is set by the luminance range, room reflections included: 50 cd/m² of ambient light halves a 0.5–400 cd/m² display's 615. §34.4 §34.5
  5. Scan conversion: Envelope detection roughly doubles the lateral bandwidth, so a conservative design guide is detected lines twice as dense as IQ lines: angular spacing \(\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{det}} \le \lambda/(4D)\), with λ the wavelength and D the width of an unapodized aperture. Sparser lines leave radial bands and dim targets between lines (7.4 dB at 1.72° spacing, 2.5 MHz, D = 20 mm). §34.6
  6. Time and persistence: Persistence blends frames, \(y_n = (1-\alpha)y_{n-1} + \alpha x_n\), with \(x_n\) the new frame and α its weight. Its lag depends on frame rate: α = 0.5 is 72 ms at 20 Hz but 14 ms at 100 Hz. It cuts electronic noise, not the speckle of a still scene, and smears motion. §34.7
  7. Colour, measurements and storage: Calipers inherit the assumed sound speed: at a true 1450 m/s axial lengths read 6.2 % long, more than pixel quantization. Stored images are 8-bit gray values after compression and gray map, and lossy JPEG smooths speckle, so texture and quantitative work need uncompressed data, preferably IQ. §34.8 §34.10 §34.11
From the field
  • Design presets on a DICOM-calibrated (GSDF) display, then check them under the room light of use. An uncalibrated gamma-2.2 monitor spreads just-noticeable differences (JNDs) unevenly, so a map tuned on it fits its quirks. Room light removes dark-end JNDs: a 0.5–400 cd/m² display drops from 615 to 302 at 50 cd/m² ambient. §34.4 §34.5
  • Judge the look on the hospital (PACS) reading station, not only on the scanner. Images are stored as 8-bit gray values after compression and gray map, then shown through the station's own display function; lossy JPEG smooths speckle, and the DICOM lossy-compression flag (0028,2110), once set, is never reset. §34.4 §34.11
  • Make calipers use the calibration of the region they sit in, refreshed on every depth or zoom change. DICOM stores mm per pixel per ultrasound region, and pixel quantization is small; sound speed dominates: an assumed 1540 m/s reads axial lengths 6.2 % long at a true 1450 m/s, 1.2 mm on 20 mm. §34.10
  • Specify persistence as a time constant; recompute the frame weight α when frame rate changes. A depth or sector change alters frame rate and so, silently, the lag. Holding a 100 ms time constant needs α = 0.39 at 20 Hz and 0.095 at 100 Hz. §34.7
  • Run quantitative measurements on IQ data, never on the displayed or exported image. Adaptive gain, clipping and lossy compression change the gray-to-echo mapping. Check histogram-overlap gCNR on IQ against the exported image: display processing may change the contrast-to-noise ratio but should not lower gCNR beyond what its clipping explains. §34.2 §34.12

34.1The chain, stage by stage, and its bit budget

After beamforming, a B-mode system does roughly this to every sample:

  1. Detect the envelope (Module 08).
  2. Apply time-gain compensation (TGC) and gain.
  3. Compress the amplitude range.
  4. Apply a gray map.
  5. Scan convert from beam lines to pixels.
  6. Filter over frames (persistence).
  7. Write 8 or 10 bits per pixel into a frame buffer.
  8. Send them over a video link to a monitor, whose lookup table and panel turn them into luminance.
  9. Add the room light reflected by the screen.
  10. Hand the result to a visual system that can tell a few hundred luminance levels apart.

The first design question is how many bits and decibels each stage needs.

\[ \mathrm{SNR}_q = 6.02\,b + 1.76\ \ \mathrm{dB}, \qquad \sigma_e^2 = \frac{\Delta^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta = \frac{2V_{\mathrm{FS}}}{2^{b}} \]
(34.1)
\(\mathrm{SNR}_q\)
signal-to-quantization-noise ratio for a full-scale sine, dB
\(b\)

\(\Delta\)

\(V_{\mathrm{FS}}\)

\(\sigma_e^2\)

12 bits: 74.0 dB; 14 bits: 86.0 dB. This is the instantaneous range of one sample. Analog TGC moves that window with depth, so the range of echo levels across a frame can be much larger than 6.02\(b\).
\[ \mathrm{SNR}_N = \mathrm{SNR}_1 + 10\log_{10}N\ \ \mathrm{dB}, \qquad b_\Sigma = b_{\mathrm{ADC}} + \lceil \log_2 N \rceil \]
(34.2)
\(N\)

128 channels: +21.1 dB and +7 bits, so a 12-bit ADC gives 19-bit sums. Apodization lowers the coherent gain; correlated noise (clutter, reverberation) gains as much as the signal and is not reduced by summation.

Detection and compression then reduce the word length. With log compression over a displayed dynamic range DR into \(b_o\) output bits:

\[ D = 20\log_{10}\frac{A}{A_{\mathrm{ref}}}, \qquad P = \operatorname{round}\!\left[(2^{b_o} - 1)\,\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\frac{D + \mathrm{DR}}{\mathrm{DR}},\,0,\,1\right)\right], \qquad \Delta_{\mathrm{dB}} = \frac{\mathrm{DR}}{2^{b_o} - 1}, \qquad \sigma_q = \frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{dB}}}{\sqrt{12}} \]

60 dB in 8 bits: 0.235 dB per level and \(\sigma_q\) = 0.068 dB, against the 5.57 dB spread of log-compressed speckle (Module 26): the quantization variance is 0.015% of the speckle variance. Speckle also dithers the quantizer, so a mean difference much smaller than one level survives on average over a region. On the display side, a 0.5–400 cd/m² GSDF monitor at 0.2 cd/m² ambient realizes 615 JNDs (Section 34.4): 2.41 JND per 8-bit step, 0.60 per 10-bit step.

So the 8-bit log stage is rarely the limit for speckle images; the limits are the choice of DR and gray map (what gets clipped), and what the monitor and the room do with the 256 levels. 34.2Compression design: log, μ-law, gain, TGC and the noise floor

Echo amplitudes in one frame span far more than a display can show: specular interfaces sit tens of decibels above parenchymal scatter, and attenuation adds tens more with depth. The log map is the natural compressor for speckle because it turns every multiplicative factor (gain, attenuation, a reflectivity ratio) into a shift and gives fully developed speckle the same 5.57 dB spread everywhere (Module 26), so texture looks the same at every brightness.

\[ G_{\mathrm{TGC}}(z) = 2\,\alpha_0\, f_0\, z\ \ \mathrm{dB}, \qquad \frac{N_{\mathrm{floor}}}{S}(z) = \frac{N_{\mathrm{floor}}}{S}(0) + 2\,\alpha_0\, f_0\, z\ \ \mathrm{dB} \]
(34.4)
\(G_{\mathrm{TGC}}(z)\)
gain that compensates two-way attenuation at depth \(z\), dB
\(\alpha_0\)

\(f_0\)

\(z\)

\(N_{\mathrm{floor}}/S\)

0.5 dB/(cm·MHz) at 5 MHz to 15 cm: 75 dB. Noise enters after attenuation, so full compensation lifts the noise floor relative to tissue by the same amount. In m34_display_chain.m (3 MHz, 1–5 cm) the floor rises from −45 to −33 dB re speckle; with the automatic gain of (34.5) the black level sits 0.55 × 60 = 33 dB below the tissue median at DR 60, so the deep noise reaches the bottom of the gray scale, while at DR 30 it stays black. Placing the floor just below −DR at the deepest depth of interest is the usual design target. In practice depth-dependent attenuation is not known, which is why TGC is a user control and why automatic TGC methods estimate it from the data.

Gain sets \(A_{\mathrm{ref}}\) in (34.3). One simple automatic rule, used in the demos and the lab, places the median tissue level at a chosen gray fraction:

\[ 20\log_{10}A_{\mathrm{ref}} = D_{50} + \mathrm{DR}\,(1 - g_t) \]
(34.5)
\(D_{50}\)

\(A_{\mathrm{ref}},\ \mathrm{DR}\)

Derivation: (34.3) with \(A = 10^{D_{50}/20}\) gives a gray fraction \(1 + (D_{50} - 20\log_{10}A_{\mathrm{ref}})/\mathrm{DR}\); set it to \(g_t\). Histogram-based or region-based automatic gain in products is more elaborate; any rule that changes \(A_{\mathrm{ref}}\) per region makes gray levels incomparable between regions.

μ-law companding, the classic telephony alternative, is linear near zero and logarithmic above, so the floor fades out smoothly instead of being clipped:

\[ y = \frac{\ln(1 + \mu x)}{\ln(1 + \mu)},\ \ x = \min\!\left(1, \frac{A}{A_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right); \qquad \mu x \gg 1:\ y \approx 1 + \frac{\ln x}{\ln(1 + \mu)}, \qquad \mu x \ll 1:\ y \approx \frac{\mu x}{\ln(1 + \mu)} \]
(34.6)
\(y\)

\(x\)

\(\mu\)

\(A,\ A_{\mathrm{ref}}\)

In its log region (using \(\ln\mu \approx \ln(1 + \mu)\)) a μ-law map behaves like a log display whose black end would lie near \(x = 1/(1 + \mu)\), i.e. a span of about \(20\log_{10}(1 + \mu)\) = 48.2 dB for \(\mu\) = 255; the approximation fails near that end, where the curve turns linear. Exact values: \(x\) = 0.1 gives 0.591 (log approximation 0.585), \(x\) = 0.01 gives 0.229 (approximation 0.170).

Adaptive dynamic range (depth- or region-dependent DR and gain, local contrast enhancement) lets every region use the full gray scale. It also breaks the one-to-one relation between gray value and echo level that a gray bar implies, so quantitative work (speckle statistics, QUS, contrast measurements) must use the IQ data, not the display.

\[ \mathrm{gCNR}\big(T(A)\big) = \mathrm{gCNR}(A)\ \ \text{for strictly monotone } T, \qquad \mathrm{gCNR}\big(Q(A)\big) \le \mathrm{gCNR}(A)\ \ \text{for any map } Q \]
(34.7)
\(\mathrm{gCNR}\)
generalized CNR, one minus the overlap of the inside and outside pixel-value distributions (Module 26)
\(T\)

\(Q\)

\(A\)

gCNR is the total-variation distance between two distributions, which no deterministic map can increase; one-to-one maps preserve it (Module 26, (26.14)). CNR has no such property. m34_display_chain.m: DR 60 → 20 dB lowers the cyst's gCNR from 0.982 to 0.937 and its gray-level CNR from 4.64 to 2.08; an S-curve gives CNR 3.31 with gCNR 0.984; for the −6 dB lesion gCNR stays 0.44 in every case (0.432 at 4 bits) while its gray-level CNR moves between 0.69 and 0.80.
Pitfall

"Better contrast" from a new compression or gray map is a display decision, not an imaging improvement, unless gCNR (or a task-based observer study) improves too. Report CNR on the linear envelope, with its definition, as Module 26 does.

Key takeaways
  • Full TGC lifts the noise floor with the tissue (75 dB at 0.5 dB/(cm·MHz), 5 MHz, 15 cm); the design target is a floor just below −DR at the deepest depth of interest.
  • μ-law with μ = 255 acts like a log display of about 48.2 dB that fades linearly into the floor instead of clipping it.
  • No deterministic map can raise gCNR and one-to-one maps keep it; clipping lowers it (cyst 0.982 → 0.937 for DR 60 → 20 dB) while CNR moves freely (4.64 → 2.08).
  • Adaptive DR or gain breaks the gray-to-echo mapping: speckle statistics, QUS and contrast measurements must use IQ data.

34.3Gray maps and detectability

A gray map is the post-processing curve from compressed to displayed value, usually a per-preset lookup table. Three families cover most of them:

\[ M_\gamma(x) = x^{\gamma_m}, \qquad M_S(x) = \frac{\tanh\big(s(x - x_0)\big) - \tanh(-s x_0)}{\tanh\big(s(1 - x_0)\big) - \tanh(-s x_0)}, \qquad M_r(x) = \max\!\left(0,\ \frac{x - x_r}{1 - x_r}\right) \]
(34.8)
\(x\)
compressed value, 0 … 1 (the clipped fraction in (34.3))
\(M_\gamma,\ \gamma_m\)
power-law map and its exponent (\(\gamma_m < 1\) lifts dark levels)
\(M_S,\ s,\ x_0\)
S-curve, its steepness and centre
\(M_r,\ x_r\)
reject map and its threshold (levels below \(x_r\) become black)
\(\tanh\)
hyperbolic tangent
The demos use \(\gamma_m\) = 0.6, \(s\) = 4, \(x_0\) = 0.5, \(x_r\) = 0.15. All three map 0 to 0 and 1 to 1. \(M_\gamma\) and \(M_S\) are one-to-one (gCNR unchanged); \(M_r\) merges everything below \(x_r\).

What a gray map changes is how many JNDs a given echo difference becomes on the screen. For a lesion whose mean log level is \(\lvert C\rvert\) dB below a background displayed at gray fraction \(x_b\) (small contrast, no clipping):

(34.9)

GSDF display, linear map, DR 60 dB, 615 JNDs: 10.3 JND per decibel; a −6 dB lesion gives 61.5 JND (61.7 measured in m34_display_chain.m). The speckle texture itself spans \(5.57\cdot 615/60\) ≈ 57 JND rms, so the observer must average over the lesion area, which is why detection is normally limited by speckle, not by the display.
\[ d' \approx C_I\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{cells}}}, \qquad C_I = 1 - 10^{-\lvert C\rvert/10}, \qquad N_{\mathrm{cells}} = n_c\,\frac{\pi d^2}{4} \]
(34.10)

The display limits detection only when \(\Delta J\) is small while \(d'\) is large, which happens with a wide DR, a gray map that is flat at the lesion's level (S-curve tails, reject), a gamma display near black, or bright reflected light. In Demo 34.3, for example, a 1 dB lesion in tissue displayed at 3% gray gives 10.2 JND on a 0.5–400 cd/m² GSDF display at 0.2 cd/m² ambient, 7.0 on a gamma-2.2 display in the same room, and only 0.36 on the gamma display with 50 cd/m² reflected light (5.0 if it were GSDF-calibrated for that light).

34.4What the DICOM GSDF guarantees, and what it does not

DICOM PS3.14 defines a JND as "the luminance difference of a given target under given viewing conditions that the average human observer can just perceive". The Grayscale Standard Display Function (GSDF) gives the luminance of JND index \(j\), derived from Barten's contrast-sensitivity model for a Standard Target: a 2° × 2° square holding a 4 cycles/degree sinusoidal grating, in a background at its mean luminance. Its range, 0.05 to 4000 cd/m², holds 1023 JNDs:

\[ \log_{10} L(j) = \frac{a + c\,x + e\,x^2 + g\,x^3 + m\,x^4}{1 + b\,x + d\,x^2 + f\,x^3 + h\,x^4 + k\,x^5}, \qquad x = \ln j, \quad j = 1, \dots, 1023 \]
(34.11)
\(L(j)\)

What the GSDF guarantees. Given a display's \(L'_{\min}\) and \(L'_{\max}\), equal P-value steps are equally perceptible for the Standard Target. An image therefore looks alike on calibrated displays with similar luminance ranges, including the effect of the stated ambient light.

What it does not.

  1. It does not say how modality values become P-values: PS3.14 "does not define how the particular picture element values in a specific imaging modality are to be presented". Compression and gray map remain the scanner's job.
  2. It does not equalize displays with different luminance ranges: 302 and 626 JNDs are both GSDF-conformant.
  3. It has no normative requirement for colour images; colour displays may be calibrated to it for grayscale images.
  4. It says nothing about uniformity, viewing angle, resolution, noise or latency.
  5. It does not set tolerances: PS3.14 "does not define when conformance … is achieved", so they come from QA documents (Section 34.12).
  6. It models a 2° target at 4 cycles/degree, not speckle texture or a small lesion in noise.

34.5Monitors and viewing conditions, including phones and tablets

The display is a luminance source with a black level, a white level, a tone curve, a spatial sampling grid and a surface that reflects the room. Each property is a design input:

  • Panel technology. Liquid-crystal panels modulate a backlight: light leakage sets their black level, and luminance and tone curve change with viewing angle (more in some liquid-crystal modes). Emissive (OLED) panels switch pixels off for a very low black level but can age unevenly under static overlays. In any lit room the reflected light \(L_{\mathrm{amb}}\), not the panel's black, usually sets \(L'_{\min}\).
  • Uniformity and warm-up. Luminance varies across the screen and drifts while the backlight stabilizes; QA measures after warm-up at several screen positions (Section 34.12).
  • Bit depth of the path. A 10-bit P-value needs a 10-bit frame buffer, a video link that carries it and a panel that resolves it; any 8-bit link in between turns the system into an 8-bit one. Calibration lookup tables need extra drive bits, (34.14).
  • Pixel pitch and viewing distance decide whether the display's sampling or the eye limits fine detail:
\[ \rho_{\mathrm{deg}} = \frac{1}{2\arctan\!\left(\dfrac{p_x}{2 d_v}\right)\cdot\dfrac{180}{\pi}} \approx \frac{\pi}{180}\,\frac{d_v}{p_x}, \qquad f_{\mathrm{Nyq}} = \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{deg}}}{2} \]
(34.17)

\(d_v\)

\(f_{\mathrm{Nyq}}\)

0.25 mm pitch at 600 mm: 41.9 px/degree (21 cycles/degree); 0.06 mm at 300 mm (a high-density phone held close): 87 px/degree. The GSDF's Standard Target is 4 cycles/degree. A speckle grain displayed 0.5 mm wide at 600 mm subtends 0.048°, about 2.9 arcmin.

Handheld systems on phones and tablets. The display is a consumer panel, with an sRGB-like tone curve (close to \(\gamma\) = 2.2), automatic brightness that moves \(L_{\max}\) with the room, glossy glass, and no calibration or QA under the manufacturer's control.   • Sector edges. Pixels outside the sector are masked; pixels straddling the edge need a defined rule (fade or hard edge) to avoid a jagged or bright border.

Key takeaways
  • One transmit per line: 15 cm and 128 lines give 40.1 Hz, 256 lines 20.1 Hz.
  • 45 Hz on a 60 Hz display shows frames for 1, 1, 2 refreshes (judder); synchronized buffer swaps prevent tearing at up to one refresh of latency. Measure end-to-end latency rather than adding datasheet numbers.
  • Persistence set in frames changes with frame rate: α = 0.5 is τ = 72 ms at 20 Hz but 14 ms at 100 Hz; holding τ = 100 ms needs α = 0.39 and 0.095.
  • Electronic noise variance falls by α/(2 − α) (one third at α = 0.5); still speckle (ρ = 1) does not fall at all, and a 20 mm/s target at 20 Hz with α = 0.2 lags 4.0 mm.

34.14How experts think about it

The expert model has three layers. In the amplitude domain, the question is what gets clipped: bits are cheap and speckle dithers quantization, so DR, reject and saturation decide what information reaches the screen, and gCNR is the bookkeeping that tells you whether a map lost any. In the luminance domain, the question is how many JNDs the display and the room realize and where the P-values put them: the GSDF spends them evenly, a gamma curve does not, and room light takes them away from the dark end. In time and geometry, the question is sampling: lines at the right density for the domain you interpolate, persistence specified in seconds, latency measured end to end. Everything else (colour, measurements, storage) is about not undoing those decisions downstream.

The 8-bit log stage costs almost nothing against speckle; what DR and reject clip costs everything.
60 dB / 255 = 0.235 dB per level\(\sigma_q\) = 0.068 dB vs 5.57 dB

Quantization noise adds 0.015% to the variance of log speckle (34.3), and the 5.57 dB speckle spread dithers the quantizer, so region means survive at sub-level precision. Clipping is different: every value below −DR becomes the same black, and no later stage can separate them again (34.7).

Holds for speckle-dominated images. Smooth, noise-free gradients (graphics, heavily filtered images) can show banding at 8 bits.
A display map can change CNR in either direction; it can never raise gCNR.

34.16Interview and qualifying-exam questions

I1How many bits do you need after log compression, and why is 8 often enough? · screen

Compare the step with the signal's own spread and with the display. 60 dB in 8 bits is 0.235 dB per level, rms quantization error 0.068 dB, against 5.57 dB for log speckle (34.3); speckle dithers the quantizer, so region means are preserved. On a 0.5–400 cd/m² GSDF display at 0.2 cd/m², 8 bits give 2.41 JND per step: adjacent levels are distinguishable, so smooth ramps can band; 10 bits give 0.60.

Follow-up: when does 10 bits matter? Smooth gradients, wide DR on a bright display, and calibration LUTs (the drive needs more bits than the P-values).

I2What does DICOM GSDF calibration guarantee, and what does it not? · screen

Guarantees: for the display's own \(L'_{\min}\) and \(L'_{\max}\) (ambient included), equal P-value steps are equal steps in JND index for the Standard Target, so images look alike across calibrated displays of similar range. Does not: define how modality values become P-values, make displays with different luminance ranges equivalent (302 and 626 JNDs are both conformant), cover colour normatively, or say anything about uniformity, angle, resolution or latency; PS3.14 does not define conformance tolerances.

Follow-up: is a GSDF display perceptually linear for ultrasound speckle? It is linear for a 2°, 4 cycles/degree grating at threshold; speckle texture and small lesions in noise are different stimuli.

I3A colleague's new gray map raises lesion CNR by 30%. Is the system better? · onsite

Not by that evidence. CNR on display values depends on the map; gCNR cannot increase under any deterministic map and is unchanged by one-to-one maps (34.7). Ask for gCNR (or an observer study) and check where the map's slope sits relative to the lesion and background levels: a steeper map at the lesion's gray level does raise \(\Delta J\) (34.9), which can matter when the display, not speckle, limits detection.

Follow-up: can a gray map ever help detection? Yes, if it moves the relevant levels out of a region where the display realizes few JNDs (near black in a bright room); it reallocates JNDs, it does not create information.

I4A 0.5–400 cd/m² monitor is moved from a reading room (0.2 cd/m² reflected) to an exam room (2 cd/m²). Quantify the change. · onsite

LR′ falls from 572 to 161 (34.16); JNDs fall from 615 to 557 (34.13), about 9%, all at the dark end. If the display was calibrated for 0.2 cd/m², its dark steps are now too close; recalibrating for 2 cd/m² restores equal spacing (2.18 JND per 8-bit step). At 50 cd/m² (bright ward, phone outdoors) only 302 JNDs remain.

Follow-up: what would you change in the image? Smaller DR or a gray map without a flat dark tail, so tissue sits away from the black end.

I5How many lines does a 2.5 MHz phased array with a 20 mm aperture need over 80°, and does it matter whether you interpolate before or after detection? · onsite

The two-way field spans \(\lvert\nu\rvert \le D/\lambda\) cycles/rad; IQ Nyquist \(\lambda/(2D)\) = 0.88° (91 lines), detected \(\lambda/(4D)\) = 0.44° (182 lines) (34.19). With fewer lines, IQ interpolation darkens speckle midway (−3 dB for independent samples) and loses points between lines; detected-domain interpolation keeps brightness but changes speckle statistics (34.20).

Follow-up: how do systems get dense lines without losing frame rate? Parallel receive beams per transmit, or plane/diverging-wave transmits (Module 23).

I6Persistence is a per-frame weight α = 0.5. The user doubles the depth. What happens and how would you redesign it? · onsite

Frame rate roughly halves (34.21), so \(\tau = -T_F/\ln(1 - \alpha)\) doubles (34.24): more smear and lag (mean lag one frame, now twice as long), while the noise reduction (\(\alpha/(2 - \alpha)\) = 1/3) is unchanged. Specify \(\tau\) and set \(\alpha = 1 - e^{-T_F/\tau}\) at every frame-rate change; for \(\tau\) = 100 ms that is 0.39 at 20 Hz and 0.095 at 100 Hz.

Follow-up: why not just use more persistence? It does not reduce static speckle at all (34.25), and it lags moving targets by \(v\,t_{\mathrm{pers}}\).

I7Your handheld probe displays on consumer phones and tablets. What is different from a cart system, and what do you design for? · research

Uncalibrated sRGB-like tone curve, automatic brightness, glossy screens, a wide range of pixel densities and room light up to daylight, and no QA. Quantify with (34.13): 50 cd/m² of reflection halves the JNDs of a 0.5–400 cd/m² display. Design: render to a known tone curve and control brightness where the platform allows, presets for bright environments (smaller DR, no dark tails), test detectability under representative lighting, and avoid relying on subtle dark-gray differences.

Follow-up: can you calibrate a phone? You can characterize a model and correct its tone curve, but auto-brightness, ambient and unit-to-unit variation make it a moving target; design margins are more robust than calibration.

I8Explain colour write priority and its failure modes. · screen

Colour is written only where flow power exceeds a threshold and the B-mode gray is below a write-priority threshold (34.26): bright, moving tissue keeps its gray, which suppresses flash. Failure modes: flow next to bright walls or in echogenic regions is hidden (threshold too low), flash appears on bright tissue (too high), and noise appears when the power threshold is low.

Follow-up: which colour map would you choose? One with monotonic lightness in each direction and a sign coded by hue (red/blue), checked under CVD simulation (34.27), (34.28).

I9A 20 mm structure lies under a thick layer of fat. Estimate the caliper error and say what the simple formula misses. · onsite

If the whole path were 1450 m/s, axial distances would read \(1540/1450\) = 1.062 times true: 21.24 mm (34.31). With a fat layer over 1540 m/s tissue, a depth inside the structure is shifted by the layer's time error, but the structure's own axial length is scaled by its own speed, not the fat's. Refraction at the layer and defocus also act (Module 32).

Follow-up: lateral lengths? Unchanged for an unsteered linear array; steered beams go to \(\sin\theta = (c/c_0)\sin\theta_0\).

I10Should cine loops be archived with lossy compression? · research

A 10 s RGB loop at 30 Hz and 600 × 800 is 432 MB uncompressed (34.33), so lossy compression is common for colour cine. Check: the clinical task (small lesions and texture suffer first), the colour sampling (YBR_FULL_422 halves horizontal chroma), and that the lossy flag (0028,2110) is set. Never use lossy data for speckle statistics or QUS; store B-mode-only loops as MONOCHROME2 (one third of RGB).

Follow-up: what do you keep for research? IQ or pre-persistence data with its processing state documented.

I11Write a latency budget for an interventional (needle-guidance) product. · research

\(t_{\mathrm{lat}} = t_{\mathrm{acq}} + t_{\mathrm{proc}} + t_{\mathrm{pers}} + t_{\mathrm{buf}} + t_{\mathrm{panel}}\) (34.23). With 30 Hz frames, a one-frame pipeline, \(\alpha\) = 0.5 persistence, one or two 60 Hz refreshes and a few milliseconds of panel: about 120–140 ms (illustrative assumptions). Levers: higher frame rate (fewer lines, smaller sector), shorter pipelines, persistence off or motion adaptive during needle motion, refresh-synchronized single buffering, a fast panel. Measure end to end with a photodiode or high-speed camera.

Follow-up: why does the frame cadence matter too? Uneven display of frames (34.22) makes needle motion look jerky even at acceptable average latency.

34.18Self-check

Q1DR 70 dB into 8 bits: decibels per level and rms quantization error?

70/255 = 0.275 dB per level; \(\sigma_q\) = 0.275/\(\sqrt{12}\) = 0.079 dB (34.3), negligible against 5.57 dB speckle.

Q2A display reaches L′min = 1 and L′max = 350 cd/m² with the room light included. How many JNDs, and how many per 8-bit step?

\(j(350) - j(1)\) = 581.6 JNDs (34.12), (34.13); 2.28 per 8-bit step on a GSDF-calibrated display.

Q3You want τ = 50 ms at 40 Hz. Which α, how many effective frames for noise, and what mean lag?

\(\alpha = 1 - e^{-25/50}\) = 0.393; \(N_{\mathrm{eff}} = (2 - \alpha)/\alpha\) = 4.1; lag \((1 - \alpha)T_F/\alpha\) = 38.5 ms (34.24), (34.25).

Q4Maximum frame rate at 12 cm depth with 128 lines, one transmit per line? With two focal zones?

1540/(2 × 0.12 × 128) = 50.1 Hz; with two transmits per line 25.1 Hz (34.21).

Q5A structure measures 30.0 mm axially; the true speed in it is 1580 m/s. True length?

\(z = (c/c_0)\hat z\) = 30.0 × 1580/1540 = 30.78 mm (34.31): the scanner read 2.5% short.

Q6Size of a 5 s, 50 Hz, 480 × 640 MONOCHROME2 loop, uncompressed?

480 × 640 × 1 × 250 × 1 byte = 76.8 MB (34.33); RGB would be 230 MB.

Q7Pixels per degree for a 0.3 mm pitch monitor viewed at 700 mm?

\((\pi/180)(700/0.3)\) = 40.7 px/degree (34.17), a Nyquist limit of 20 cycles/degree.

Q8Is the cyst gCNR measured on a scanner's DICOM export a valid measure of beamformer quality?

Only as a lower bound: the export has been compressed, clipped, gray-mapped, scan converted and possibly lossy compressed, and each many-to-one step can lower gCNR (34.7); scan conversion and persistence also change the pixel distributions. Measure on IQ data for the beamformer, on the export for the whole chain.
