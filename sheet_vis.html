0Convolution and correlation, visually

Modules 03 (convolution), 11 (correlation and delay estimation)

Both operations slide one function past the other, multiply, and integrate. Convolution flips g first; correlation does not. That single flip is why convolution models a system (output = input ∗ impulse response) while correlation measures similarity (where does g best match f?).

\[ (f \ast g)(t) = \int f(\tau)\, g(t - \tau)\, d\tau \;\longleftrightarrow\; F(\nu)\, G(\nu) \]
Convolution: flip g, shift it right by t, multiply, integrate. Commutative: \(f \ast g = g \ast f\).
\[ (f \star g)(t) = \int f(\tau)\, g^*(\tau - t)\, d\tau = \int f(u + t)\, g^*(u)\, du \;\longleftrightarrow\; F(\nu)\, G^*(\nu) \]
Cross-correlation: shift g right by t without flipping. Same convention as MATLAB xcorr(f, g). Not commutative: \((g \star f)(t) = (f \star g)^*(-t)\). Correlation = convolution with the conjugated, time-reversed g.
\[ (f \star f)(t) \;\longleftrightarrow\; \lvert F(\nu)\rvert^2 \]
Autocorrelation: Hermitian symmetric, maximum at t = 0 where it equals the energy \(\int \lvert f\rvert^2\) (Wiener–Khinchin).

Conventions differ

Some texts (and the common Wikipedia figure) define \((f \star g)(t) = \int f^*(\tau)\, g(\tau + t)\, d\tau\), which is the curve above mirrored in t and conjugated. Check the convention before reading a sign off a lag: it decides whether a positive lag means "g is late" or "g is early".
