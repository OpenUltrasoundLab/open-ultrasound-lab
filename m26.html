Speckle statistics and image-quality metrics

Speckle is not noise to be wished away; it is a random field whose statistics are fixed by the scatterers and the PSF. Know its distributions, its size and what compounding does to it, and you can measure image quality without fooling yourself or a reviewer.

You will be able to

  • Derive the Rayleigh numbers (\(\mathrm{SNR}_0\) = 1.91, intensity contrast 1, log std 5.57 dB, log-mean offset −2.51 dB) and use them as calibration checks
  • Read envelope statistics: K, Rician, Nakagami \(m\), homodyned K, and how many scatterers make speckle fully developed
  • Predict speckle size from the PSF (Siegert relation) and the gain of incoherent vs coherent compounding from k-space overlap
  • Report contrast, CNR (with a stated definition) and gCNR, and know which ones a display map can inflate
  • Set detection thresholds in Rayleigh and heavier-tailed backgrounds (ULM)

In 60 seconds
  1. Fully developed speckle: With many random scatterers per resolution cell, the envelope is Rayleigh and intensity exponential, so ratios of their statistics are fixed numbers independent of gain. The envelope mean over its standard deviation, \(\mathrm{SNR}_0 = \sqrt{\pi/(4-\pi)} = 1.91\), is the standard pipeline check. Measure it on beamformed IQ or the linear envelope, never the log image. §26.1
  2. Beyond Rayleigh: A steady coherent echo in the cell (Rician statistics) narrows the amplitude spread and pushes the envelope mean-to-spread ratio above 1.91; sparse scatterers or a fluctuating local mean (K distribution) fatten the tail and push it below. The two effects can cancel, so one ratio cannot tell which model applies. §26.2
  3. Scatterer density: Amplitude statistics approach Rayleigh slowly as scatterers per cell increase, and are noisy, so they measure density poorly. The Nakagami shape m (1 for Rayleigh speckle) scatters as \(\mathrm{std}\,\hat m \approx 2/\sqrt n\) over n independent cells. Telling 10 scatterers per cell (m = 0.935) from fully developed speckle needs more than 3800 cells. §26.3
  4. Speckle size is the PSF: Speckle grain size is set by the imaging system's point-spread function (PSF), not by the tissue. Axial grain width at half maximum is 0.441 c/B, with c the sound speed and B the bandwidth: 0.226 mm at 5 MHz and 60 % bandwidth. Measure on intensity or IQ; log compression and scan conversion change it. §26.4
  5. Compounding: Averaging detected images from different angles or sub-apertures smooths speckle only as far as the looks decorrelate: speckle contrast falls as \(C = 1/\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{eff}}}\), with \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) the effective number of independent looks. Nine looks with intensity correlation 0.25 count as three. Summing IQ before detection keeps speckle Rayleigh, making it finer rather than smoother. §26.5
  6. Image metrics: The contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR) depends on the representation and, on a dB image, grows with the displayed dynamic range. gCNR, one minus the overlap of the two regions' pixel histograms, is unchanged by any strictly monotone display map, so use it to compare processing; independent speckle cells, not pixels, set its precision. §26.6
  7. Detection: Speckle and noise have exponential intensity, so the threshold for a chosen per-pixel false-alarm probability \(P_{fa}\) follows directly: \(T = -\ln P_{fa}\,\langle I\rangle\), with \(\langle I\rangle\) the mean background intensity. For \(P_{fa} = 10^{-6}\) it sits 11.4 dB above the mean. Heavier-tailed backgrounds cross it far more often, so normalize by a local background estimate first. §26.7
From the field
  • Check the first-order speckle numbers on a phantom before you trust a pipeline. Fully developed speckle gives an envelope mean-to-spread ratio of 1.91 and a 5.57 dB log spread at any gain, so a departure on a speckle phantom flags processing. Measure on IQ or the linear envelope, not the display. §26.1
  • Compare processing with gCNR, not CNR, and quote the representation and gray map. gCNR, one minus the overlap of the two regions' histograms, ignores any monotone map; the contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR) on a dB image follows the display: the same anechoic cyst reads 6.7 at 40 dB dynamic range and 10.3 at 60 dB. §26.6
  • Size regions in speckle cells, not pixels, and report the null value. Pixels within one resolution cell are correlated, so cell count sets the noise floor. At 5 MHz, 60 % bandwidth and F-number 2 (about 7.5 cells/mm²) a 2.5 mm cyst region holds 95 cells and gives histogram-overlap gCNR ≈ 0.1 between identical regions. §26.6
  • Do not infer scatterer density from one statistic. A coherent echo and sparse scatterers push the statistics opposite ways and can cancel; the Nakagami shape m (1 for Rayleigh speckle) scatters as 2/√n over n cells. Separating 10 scatterers per cell (m = 0.935) from m = 1 at two standard deviations needs 3800+ cells. §26.2 §26.3
  • Normalize by a local background estimate before setting a detection threshold. A threshold derived for exponential intensity assumes Rayleigh speckle; heavier-tailed K-distributed backgrounds exceed it far more often. At the Rayleigh threshold for a 10⁻⁶ false-alarm rate, a K background with shape α = 1 gives a false-alarm rate of 2.1×10⁻³. §26.7

26.1Fully developed speckle: the numbers that follow from one assumption

Module 07 showed that the IQ sample \(z\) of a resolution cell that holds many randomly placed scatterers is circular complex Gaussian. Write \(\langle I\rangle = \mathrm{E}\lvert z\rvert^2\) for the mean intensity. Everything in this section follows from that one assumption, and every number is a calibration check to run on a phantom before you trust a pipeline.

\[ \begin{aligned} p_R(R) &= \frac{2R}{\langle I\rangle}\, e^{-R^2/\langle I\rangle}\ \ (\text{Rayleigh}), \qquad \mathrm{E}R = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sqrt{\langle I\rangle} = 0.886\sqrt{\langle I\rangle}, \qquad \mathrm{E}R^2 = \langle I\rangle \\ \mathrm{var}\,R &= \left(1 - \frac{\pi}{4}\right)\langle I\rangle = (0.463)^2\langle I\rangle \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \mathrm{SNR}_0 = \frac{\mathrm{E}R}{\mathrm{std}\,R} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{4 - \pi}} = 1.913 \end{aligned} \]

Exponential intensity: the brightest speckle in a region of \(n\) independent cells is about \(\ln n\) times the mean (13.8 for \(n = 10^6\)), which matters for thresholds (Section 26.7).

The log-intensity follows a (reversed) Gumbel / Fisher–Tippett law: skewed, with a long tail toward dark values and the mode at 0 dB. Gain shifts \(D\), so the 5.57 dB spread is gain independent. Module 08 quotes the 5.57 dB; the −2.51 dB offset is the one that biases contrast measurements.
Pitfall

All of these numbers hold for the linear envelope of unprocessed, fully developed speckle. Scanner B-mode pixels are log compressed, clipped, spatially filtered and scan converted: measuring \(\mathrm{SNR}_0\) on them gives values far from 1.91 even in a perfect phantom. Compute speckle statistics on beamformed IQ (or its envelope) before display processing.

Key takeaways
  • Circular Gaussian IQ gives a Rayleigh envelope with SNR₀ = √(π/(4−π)) = 1.913 and an exponential intensity with CI = 1; both ratios cancel gain, TGC and transmit power.
  • Log-compressed speckle has std 5.57 dB and a mean 2.51 dB below 10 log₁₀⟨I⟩; the offset biases contrasts taken as differences of mean dB values.
  • Measure on beamformed IQ or the linear envelope: log compression, clipping, filtering and scan conversion move SNR₀ far from 1.91 even in a perfect phantom.

26.2Beyond Rayleigh: K, Rician, Nakagami and homodyned K

Two departures from fully developed speckle are common in tissue, and they push the statistics in opposite directions.

  • Too few scatterers per cell, or a fluctuating local mean (pre-Rayleigh): heavier tail, more dark and more very bright pixels, \(C_I > 1\), \(\mathrm{SNR}_0 < 1.91\).
  • A coherent component (post-Rayleigh): a specular interface, a regular scatterer arrangement or a strong point target adds a steady phasor \(A\); the spread narrows, \(C_I < 1\), \(\mathrm{SNR}_0 > 1.91\).

A simple generative model contains both. In one resolution cell there are a Poisson number of scatterers (mean \(\bar N\)), each with a random phase and an amplitude \(a\) (\(\mathrm{E}a^2 = 1\), \(\kappa = \mathrm{E}a^4/(\mathrm{E}a^2)^2\): 1 for equal amplitudes, 2 for Rayleigh-distributed amplitudes), plus a coherent phasor \(A\). With diffuse power \(P = \bar N\) and \(r = A^2/P\):

\[ \begin{aligned} z &= A + \sum_{i=1}^{N} a_i\, e^{j\varphi_i}, \qquad N \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\bar N) \\ \langle I\rangle &= A^2 + P, \qquad \mathrm{var}\,I = P^2 + 2A^2P + \kappa\bar N \\ m &\equiv \frac{\langle I\rangle^2}{\mathrm{var}\,I} = \frac{(1 + r)^2}{1 + 2r + \kappa/\bar N} \end{aligned} \]

26.3How many scatterers make speckle fully developed?

A resolution cell is not a box: each scatterer at \(x_i\) contributes with the PSF weight \(h(x_i - x_0)\). For a Poisson process of density \(n\) (scatterers per mm² in 2D), Campbell's theorem gives the first two intensity moments directly:

\[ \begin{aligned} \langle I\rangle &= n \int \lvert h\rvert^2, \qquad \mathrm{E}I^2 - 2\langle I\rangle^2 = \kappa\, n \int \lvert h\rvert^4 \\ \Rightarrow\;\; C_I^2 &= 1 + \frac{\kappa}{N_c}, \qquad N_c = \frac{n \left(\int \lvert h\rvert^2\right)^2}{\int \lvert h\rvert^4} \end{aligned} \]

26.9How experts think about it

The expert model has three layers. First, fully developed speckle is a circular Gaussian field, so its first-order statistics are fixed numbers (1.91, 1, 5.57 dB, −2.51 dB) and any departure is information: about scatterer density, coherent structure, or your processing. Second, its second-order statistics are the PSF: speckle size, decorrelation and compounding gain all follow from overlaps in k-space. Third, an image-quality metric is a statistic of two distributions, and only metrics invariant to the display map (gCNR) or computed on a stated, linear representation can be compared between papers.

26.11Interview and qualifying-exam questions

I1Derive the speckle SNR of 1.91. What do you need to assume? · screen

Many scatterers with independent uniform phases per cell: by the central limit theorem \(z\) is circular complex Gaussian with \(\mathrm{E}\lvert z\rvert^2 = \langle I\rangle\). Then \(R = \lvert z\rvert\) is Rayleigh, \(\mathrm{E}R = (\sqrt{\pi}/2)\sqrt{\langle I\rangle}\), \(\mathrm{E}R^2 = \langle I\rangle\), \(\mathrm{var}\,R = (1 - \pi/4)\langle I\rangle\), so \(\mathrm{SNR}_0 = \sqrt{\pi/4}/\sqrt{1 - \pi/4} = \sqrt{\pi/(4-\pi)}\) = 1.913 (26.1). Intensity is exponential with contrast 1.

Follow-up: what is it on the intensity, and on the log image? Intensity mean/std = 1; log image std 5.57 dB, independent of gain.

I2Why is the standard deviation of log-compressed speckle 5.57 dB whatever the gain, and what is its mean? · screen

For exponential \(I\), \(u = \ln(I/\langle I\rangle)\) has a fixed (Gumbel) distribution with mean \(-\gamma\) and variance \(\pi^2/6\). Gain multiplies \(I\), which adds a constant to \(u\). Times \(10/\ln 10\): std = \(4.343\cdot\pi/\sqrt{6}\) = 5.57 dB, mean = −2.51 dB re \(10\log_{10}\langle I\rangle\) (26.3).

Follow-up: so how should you compute lesion contrast from a dB image? From mean intensities (linearize first), or accept the −2.51 dB offset cancels only if both regions are Rayleigh.

I3You measure \(\mathrm{SNR}_0\) = 2.4 in a liver ROI. List the explanations in the order you would check them. · onsite

(1) Processing: was it computed on the linear envelope, before log compression, spatial filtering, persistence or scan conversion? Smoothing raises \(\mathrm{SNR}_0\). (2) Compounding: incoherent compounding raises it by \(\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{eff}}}\). (3) Physics: a coherent component (specular structures, regular spacing), Rician-like; \(\mathrm{SNR}_0\) = 2.4 matches a Rician \(r \approx 1.75\) (2.4 dB), i.e. \(m \approx 1.7\) (invert a measured \(\hat m\) with \(r = m - 1 + \sqrt{m^2 - m}\)). (4) Check the same pipeline on a phantom: it must read \(1.91 \pm 1.36/\sqrt{n}\).

Follow-up: how would you tell coherent structure from smoothing? Smoothing widens the autocovariance beyond \(\lvert\mu\rvert^2\) of the PSF; a coherent component does not.

I4What is the speckle size at 5 MHz, 60% bandwidth, F#2, and how many independent cells are in a 10 mm × 10 mm ROI? · onsite

Axial FWHM of \(\lvert\mu\rvert^2\) = \(0.441\,c/B\) = 0.441 × 1540/3×10⁶ = 0.226 mm. Lateral (rect, focal zone) \(0.835\,\lambda F_\#\) = 0.835 × 0.308 × 2 = 0.514 mm. Correlation area \(\iint\lvert\mu\rvert^2\) = 0.241 × 0.554 mm², i.e. 7.5 cells/mm², so about 750 cells in 1 cm². \(\mathrm{SNR}_0\) from that ROI scatters by about \(1.36/\sqrt{750}\) = 0.05.

Follow-up: what changes off focus? The lateral size follows the local beam width, so the count falls with distance from focus.

I5Why does coherent plane-wave compounding not reduce speckle, while spatial compounding does? · screen

Coherent compounding sums IQ: the result is a linear combination of circular Gaussian fields, hence circular Gaussian, so \(C_I = 1\) and \(\mathrm{SNR}_0\) = 1.91. It widens the k-space support, so speckle gets finer and resolution and clutter improve. Spatial compounding averages detected images from looks with low k-space overlap, so contrast falls as \(1/\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{eff}}}\) (26.11).

Follow-up: does coherent compounding change gCNR of a cyst? Yes, through contrast: lower sidelobes and clutter mean less energy in the cyst, so a higher intensity ratio; at a given ratio, gCNR is the same function of \(r\).

I6Three incoherently compounded plane-wave angles at −10°, 0°, +10°, receive F# 1.5. How many effective looks? · onsite

Field correlation \(r = 1 - \lvert\Delta\sin\theta\rvert\cdot F_\#\) (26.13): adjacent pairs \(\Delta\sin\theta\) = 0.174, \(r\) = 0.74, \(\rho_I\) = 0.55; outer pair \(\Delta\sin\theta\) = 0.347, \(r\) = 0.48, \(\rho_I\) = 0.23. \(\sum\rho_I\) = 3 + 2(0.55 + 0.55 + 0.23) = 5.65, so \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) = 9/5.65 = 1.6 and \(C_I\) = 0.79, not 0.58.

Follow-up: what angle span gives independent looks? \(\Delta\sin\theta \ge 1/F_\#\) = 0.67 between neighbours, about 40°, which is why speckle reduction needs large angles or sub-aperture translation of half the aperture or more.

I7A reviewer notes that your CNR rose 40% between two figures and the only change was the display dynamic range. Respond. · onsite

The reviewer is right that a dB-image CNR depends on the clipping floor: for an anechoic cyst it is ≈ \((\mathrm{DR} - 2.51)/5.57\). State the CNR definition (we use the linear envelope, pooled variance), recompute on the envelope, which has a ceiling of 1.91 for an anechoic cyst in fully developed speckle, and add gCNR, which is invariant to strictly monotone display maps.

Follow-up: can gCNR also be gamed? Not by the display map; it can be changed by ROI choice, bin rule and sample size (positive bias around 0.1 with about 100 cells), and by beamformers that change the output distribution's shape.

I8Define gCNR, give its operational meaning and its value for a −6 dB lesion in fully developed speckle. · research

\(\mathrm{gCNR} = 1 - \int\min(p_{\mathrm{in}}, p_{\mathrm{out}})\), the total-variation distance between the pixel-value distributions inside and outside. An ideal pixel classifier with equal priors is correct with probability \((1 + \mathrm{gCNR})/2\). For two exponential intensities with ratio \(r = 10^{0.6}\): \(\mathrm{gCNR} = r^{-1/(r-1)}(1 - 1/r)\) = 0.471 (classifier 74% correct).

Follow-up: how do you estimate it? Histograms with a fixed rule (quantile bins of the pooled data make it exactly display invariant), enough independent cells, and a null measurement on two same-statistics regions.

I9You want a Nakagami-m image of liver with ±0.05 precision. What window size, and what limits it? · research

\(\mathrm{std}\,\hat m \approx 2/\sqrt{n}\), so \(n\) ≈ 1600 independent cells. At 7.5 cells/mm² (5 MHz, 60%, F#2) that is about 210 mm², roughly a 15 mm window: far coarser than the B-mode. Limits: depth normalization (TGC, attenuation, diffraction) inside the window, mixtures at boundaries, small-\(n\) bias (\(\hat m\) is biased upward), and the lateral cell growing off focus.

Follow-up: how to do better? Compound independent looks or frequency bands, use 3D data, or use estimators with better efficiency (maximum likelihood) and report precision maps.

I10How should a ULM pipeline set the bubble detection threshold after SVD filtering? · research

Model the background as complex Gaussian: \(P_{fa} = e^{-T/\langle I\rangle}\), so \(T = 13.8\langle I\rangle\) (11.4 dB) for \(10^{-6}\) (26.16). Because \(\langle I\rangle\) varies with depth and region, normalize by a local noise estimate (CFAR) rather than a global threshold. Check the background histogram: residual clutter is heavier-tailed (K with \(\alpha = 1\) gives 2×10⁻³ at that threshold). Detection probability for a bubble at SNR \(S\) is Marcum Q: 0.87 at 13 dB.

Follow-up: why does a \(10^{-6}\) rate still matter? 10⁹ pixel tests per acquisition give about 1000 false detections, which tracking (track length, velocity limits) must remove.

I11How many scatterers per resolution cell do you need for fully developed speckle, and why is "10" an incomplete answer? · onsite

\(C_I^2 = 1 + \kappa/N_c\) with \(N_c\) counted in the correlation cell \((\int\lvert h\rvert^2)^2/\int\lvert h\rvert^4\), which for a Gaussian PSF is 1.44 times the −6 dB cell (26.6). Follow-up: can density alone be estimated from \(m\)? Only if there is no coherent component; otherwise use homodyned K.

26.13Self-check

Q1A phantom ROI gives envelope mean 41.2 and std 21.6 (linear units). Is the speckle fully developed?

\(\mathrm{SNR}_0\) = 41.2/21.6 = 1.91: consistent with Rayleigh. Whether 1.91 vs, say, 1.85 can be distinguished depends on the number of independent cells (std ≈ \(1.36/\sqrt{n}\)).

Q2Intensity-ratio contrast of a lesion is −6 dB. What do you get if you instead subtract mean dB values, (a) both regions Rayleigh, (b) lesion Rician with r = 10 dB?

(a) −6.0 dB: the −2.51 dB offsets cancel. (b) The lesion's log mean is −0.41 dB re its own mean intensity, so the mean-dB difference is −6 + 2.51 − 0.41 = −3.9 dB: a 2.1 dB bias.

Q3Nakagami m̂ = 0.80 from 100 independent cells. What can you conclude?

\(\mathrm{std}\,\hat m \approx 2/\sqrt{100}\) = 0.2, so 0.80 is within about one standard deviation of 1: not significantly pre-Rayleigh. With 1600 cells (std 0.05) the same value would be 4σ below 1.

Q4At 7.5 MHz, 80% bandwidth, F#1.5, rect apodization, what are the axial and lateral speckle sizes?

\(B\) = 6 MHz: axial 0.441 × 1540/6×10⁶ = 0.113 mm. \(\lambda\) = 0.205 mm, \(\lambda F_\#\) = 0.308 mm, lateral 0.835 × 0.308 = 0.257 mm.

Q5Four looks with pairwise field correlation 0.6 are averaged as intensities. What is the speckle contrast?

\(\rho_I\) = 0.36. \(N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 4/(1 + 3\times 0.36)\) = 1.92, contrast \(1/\sqrt{1.92}\) = 0.72 (independent looks would give 0.50).

Q6An anechoic cyst displayed with 40 dB dynamic range: what is the dB-image CNR, and what is the envelope CNR?

dB image: (40 − 2.51)/5.57 = 6.7 (exact clipped computation: 6.74). Envelope: 1.91, independent of the display.

Q7What threshold (re mean background intensity) gives a per-pixel false-alarm rate of \(10^{-4}\) on complex Gaussian noise?

\(T = -\ln 10^{-4}\) = 9.21\(\langle I\rangle\) = 9.6 dB.
