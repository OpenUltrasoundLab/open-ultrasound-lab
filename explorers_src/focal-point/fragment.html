The timing laws

Steering a plane wave by angle \(\theta\): element \(i\) at position \(x_i\) fires at \(t_i = t_0 + x_i \sin\theta / c\).

Focusing at a point: element \(i\) fires at \(t_i = T_F - L_i / c\), where \(L_i\) is its distance to the focus, so every wave arrives at time \(T_F\).

The echo from the focus reaches element \(i\) at \(T_F + L_i / c\); delay-and-sum removes these differences before adding.

What is exact and what is not

  • Exact: every transmit note (207 of them) starts within 0.05 ms of the delay law of its gesture; the echo and scan notes use the same geometry.
  • Scaled: the keyboard geometry and the wave speed (130 px/s) are chosen so delays last seconds and can be heard. A real probe applies nanosecond delays.
  • Free: the melody and the chords are composed freely; only the runs, sweeps and echoes are dictated by the physics.
  • Simplified: in the scan, pixels light when the transmit waves meet; the echo and delay-and-sum step is shown once, in chapter 8.
