Solver. Own broadband frequency-domain point-source solver (not Field II): every element a point source with the 3D free-space Green's function e−j2πfr/c/(4πr), no element directivity or elevation lens, Gaussian pulse spectrum on 401 frequencies; JS in Web Workers on this page. Validated against an independent Python time-domain solver (closed-form pulse superposition, no frequency grid): amplitude NRMSE of 0.04 % or less on five settings, and against Field II 3.24 for the default preset (maps NRMSE ≤ 0.34 %, −6 dB widths within 0.05 mm; Solver control).

Scenarios in words.

  1. Load (K = 1) → maps A and B are identical; Play next to K → B narrows as transmits are added; at K = 41 it stays 0.9–1.3 mm wide from 10 to 60 mm, including zf, while one transmit is 7.1 mm wide at 10 mm (C1).
  2. Delay model → virtual source only → dark notch at zf: on-axis gain −1.5 dB instead of +7.8 dB (C2).
  3. RTB weights → uniform → the row at zf widens to about 12 mm (all 41 focal spots fall inside the gate and line up side by side, a 12 mm span; they are not summed at one point).
  4. Display scaling → global maximum → a dark band in B near zf, where 3 transmits overlap instead of 41 (18 dB brightness range).
  5. Spacing s → 4 p (1.2 mm, K capped at 11) → grating lobes in B near ±7 mm at 10 mm, about −17 dB.
  6. Velocity v → +50 mm/s (d = 10 µm) → map B tilts: peak at −1.34 mm at 10 mm and +1.95 mm at 60 mm; centre-out firing removes the tilt but splits the beam.
  7. At K = 1 maps A and B are identical (same measure, same scaling); to see the energy measure differ, set weights → uniform, delay model → virtual source only, field measure → energy: a slab of about 12 mm appears at zf over three rows (29.5–30.5 mm) in energy but not in the gated measure (0.84 mm). With the default footprint weights neither measure shows a slab (energy 1.0 mm at zf), and with uniform weights and (R.1b) both do.

Symbols used here: K, number of transmits combined; s, transmit spacing; p, element pitch (0.3 mm); xk, centre of transmit k relative to the receive line; zf, transmit focal depth; Na, active elements per transmit and A = Na p the sub-aperture width; F# = zf/A, transmit F-number; an, element apodization; wk, RTB weight; f0, centre frequency, λ0 = c/f0; B, −6 dB fractional bandwidth; c = 1.54 mm/µs, true sound speed; ca = c(1 + εc), sound speed of the delay correction; d, axial displacement per transmit; |q|, envelope of the analytic pulse (gated measure); E, pulse energy (energy measure, shown as √E); SNR, signal-to-noise ratio.

A focused transmit beam is narrow only near its focus. Retrospective transmit beamforming (RTB) stores the receive data of many overlapping focused transmits and, for each receive line, adds them coherently after correcting each one's arrival time. The sum behaves like a transmit beam focused at every depth. The approach descends from synthetic transmit beams (Hergum et al. 2007) and virtual-source synthetic aperture (Frazier & O'Brien 1998; Bae & Jeong 2000); see References below. This page computes both pictures, the physical beams and the synthetic one, from the same broadband field model.

Each transmit k fires a sub-aperture centred at lateral position \(x_k\) and focused at depth \(z_f\). Below the focus its wavefront diverges as if it came from a point at the focus, the virtual source; above the focus it converges towards that point. RTB uses this picture to predict when transmit k reaches depth z on the receive line \(x = 0\):

\[ t_k(z) = \frac{z_f + \operatorname{sgn}(z - z_f)\,\sqrt{x_k^2 + (z - z_f)^2}}{c_a} \]
(R.1)
\(t_k(z)\)
predicted arrival time of transmit k at depth z on the receive line, µs
\(x_k\)
lateral centre of transmit k relative to the receive line, mm
\(z_f\)
transmit focal depth, mm
\(c_a\)
sound speed assumed by the delay correction, mm/µs
Printed with the offset \(z/c_a\) included. The computation uses \(t_k = z/c + \operatorname{sgn}(z - z_f)\,(\sqrt{x_k^2 + (z-z_f)^2} - \lvert z-z_f\rvert)/c_a\): the term \(z(1/c_a - 1/c)\), common to all transmits, only shifts the image in depth and is removed before gating (with εc ≠ 0 it would otherwise move the gate off the pulse). Virtual-source delays for synthetic aperture: Frazier & O'Brien 1998; Bae & Jeong 2000.

Shifting every transmit's receive data by \(t_k(z)\) and summing with weights \(w_k\) gives, for the transmit side of line \(x = 0\), the synthetic field

\[ Q(x,z,f) = \sum_{k} w_k\, P_k(x,z,f)\, e^{\,j 2\pi f \left[t_k(z) - 2 n_k d / c\right]}, \qquad E(x,z) = \int \lvert Q \rvert^2 \, df \]
(R.2)
\(P_k(x,z,f)\)
complex transmit field of transmit k at frequency f (the physical beam)
\(w_k\)
RTB weight of transmit k
\(n_k\)
firing index of transmit k counted from the central transmit: \(x_k/s\) for left → right firing, \(2\lvert x_k/s\rvert - [x_k > 0]\) for centre-out (0, +1, −1, +2, …)
\(d\)
axial tissue displacement between consecutive transmits, mm
\(c\)
true sound speed, 1.54 mm/µs
\(E(x,z)\)
energy of the synthetic pulse at (x, z); by Parseval equal to \(\int \lvert q(t) \rvert^2 dt\)
The correction uses the receive-line time \(t_k(z)\) for every x of a depth row, so the result is the lateral transmit profile that line x = 0 sees. Motion enters as the extra two-way delay \(2 n_k d / c\) of transmit k, with \(n_k\) its firing index counted from the central transmit. The demo shows by default the gated field \(\lvert q(x,z,t=0)\rvert\), the envelope of the corrected pulse sum at the one instant \(t = z/c\) to which the depth-z sample of line x = 0 refers; the energy E is kept as an option. Receive beamforming is not modelled: this is the transmit side only, and a focused receive aperture would sample an off-axis scatterer over a spread of transmit times (about 0.4 period for x = 0.5 mm at z = 30 mm).

Where the K transmits cover the receive line, the delays line up and the sum narrows the beam to roughly the focal width of one transmit, at every depth, provided each transmit is actually focused: this needs a Fresnel number \(N_F = A^2/(4\lambda_0 z_f)\) of about 2 or more (F# ≲ 3 at these frequencies). For weakly focused transmits (\(N_F < 1\), F# ≳ 4, reachable with the sliders) there is no focus to synthesize around: the synthetic beam is wider than one transmit over most depths and (R.3) does not apply. Near the focus the beams barely overlap, so few transmits contribute, and the virtual-source model is least accurate there: the real wavefront at the focus is nearly flat, so a neighbour at \(x_k\) actually arrives at \(z_f/c\), while (R.1) predicts \((z_f + \lvert x_k\rvert)/c_a\), an error of \(\lvert x_k\rvert/c\) (0.68 periods at 3.5 MHz for \(x_k\) = 0.3 mm), which leaves a dark notch in the synthetic beam at \(z_f\). The demo therefore uses by default a focal-zone corrected delay: the same geometry, but with the distance from the focus replaced by an interpolating radius that grows without bound at the focus. It is a heuristic interpolation between the virtual-source arrival time (far from the focus) and the plane-wave time (flat focal wavefront), with the functional form of the Gaussian-beam wavefront curvature (Kogelnik & Li 1966); it is not the measured wavefront of the simulated beam:

\[ t_k(z) = \frac{z}{c} + \operatorname{sgn}(z - z_f)\,\frac{\sqrt{x_k^2 + R^2} - R}{c_a}, \qquad R = \lvert z - z_f\rvert + \frac{z_R^2}{\lvert z - z_f\rvert}, \qquad z_R = \lambda_0 F_\#^2 \]
(R.1b)
\(R\)
interpolating radius at depth z, mm: \(\to \lvert z - z_f\rvert\) far from the focus, \(\to\infty\) at it (Gaussian-beam form \(R = z + z_R^2/z\))
\(z_R\)
tuning length, mm: the distance from the focus at which the geometric cone width \(A\lvert z - z_f\rvert/z_f\) equals \(\lambda_0 F_\#\) (the focal width without the κ = 1.21 factor). It is not the half length of the focal zone
\(F_\#\)
transmit F-number \(z_f/A\), A = sub-aperture width
\(\lambda_0\)
wavelength at the centre frequency, mm
For \(\lvert z - z_f\rvert \gg z_R\), \(R \to \lvert z - z_f\rvert\) and (R.1b) is (R.1) up to a delay common to all transmits; at the focus \(R \to \infty\), the wavefront is flat and every transmit is expected at \(z_f/c\). Defaults: \(z_R\) = 0.44 · 1.56² = 1.07 mm. The factor was chosen by scan, not derived: a Gaussian beam matched to the focal −6 dB width would have 1.65 \(\lambda_0 F_\#^2\) (1.77 mm), and the true off-axis arrival times lie between (R.1) and (R.1b) with no single \(z_R\) fitting them. Results are insensitive to it: for \(z_R\) = 0.5–2.5 \(\lambda_0 F_\#^2\) the on-axis gain at \(z_f\) stays +7.8 dB and the width 0.99 mm there, and widths elsewhere change by about 0.1 mm or less (1.13–1.24 mm at 27 mm, 1.08–1.17 mm at 35 mm).

With correct delays, the focal zone needs a second change. There, every transmit's own focal spot sits at the time the receive line samples, so uniform weights over 41 transmits add 41 spots side by side and the synthetic beam at \(z_f\) is as wide as their span. The default weights therefore follow the overlap: at each depth only the transmits whose −6 dB beam covers the line contribute (footprint weights). A delay-correction sound-speed error or tissue motion between transmits misaligns the sum and gives some of the narrowing back.

Motion between transmits. The demo moves the tissue axially (+ = away from the array) at a constant velocity v, so it is displaced by \(d = v/\mathrm{PRF}\) between consecutive transmits. When the transmits fire left to right, transmit k's echo arrives \(2 n_k d/c\) late, a delay error that grows linearly across the combined transmits. A linear delay ramp across the synthetic aperture of virtual sources (pitch s) steers the synthetic beam, which is the classic synthetic-aperture motion artefact:

\[ \sin\theta_m = \frac{2d}{s}, \qquad \Delta x(z) \approx \frac{2d}{s}\,(z - z_f) \]
(R.4)
\(\theta_m\)
tilt of the synthetic beam about the focus
\(\Delta x(z)\)
lateral shift of the synthetic beam at depth z, mm (sign flips across the focus)
\(d\)
axial displacement per transmit, mm
\(s\)
transmit spacing, mm
50 mm/s at 5 kHz gives d = 10 µm and, for s = 0.3 mm, a shift of −1.33 mm at 10 mm and +2.00 mm at 60 mm; the simulation gives −1.34 and +1.95 mm. The beam keeps its width and almost all its peak amplitude (−0.7 dB at 10 mm), but on the line x = 0 the signal drops by 26 dB because the beam has moved off it. Axial motion between acquisitions acts as a linear phase ramp across a synthetic aperture (Trahey & Nock 1992). Centre-out firing (0, +1, −1, +2, …; firing index \(2\lvert n\rvert - [n > 0]\) for transmit n) has no linear part, so the tilt disappears, but its V-shaped delay error steers the two halves of the synthetic aperture apart: the beam broadens or splits and the coherent gain is still lost.

Reading chart C1. The estimate drawn as dots treats the K virtual sources as a synthetic aperture seen from depth z. A virtual source helps only if its cone, of width \(A\lvert z - z_f\rvert / z_f\) at depth z, covers the line, so the usable synthetic aperture is the smaller of that footprint and the span \(K s\):

\[ W_{\mathrm{RTB}}(z) \approx \kappa\,\lambda_0 \,\max\!\left(\frac{z_f}{A},\; \frac{\lvert z - z_f\rvert}{K s}\right) \]
(R.3)
\(W_{\mathrm{RTB}}\)
−6 dB lateral width of the synthetic transmit beam, mm
\(\kappa\)
−6 dB width factor of the aperture weighting for continuous waves: 1.21 uniform, 2.00 Hann, 1.57 Tukey 0.5
\(\lambda_0\)
wavelength at the centre frequency, mm
\(A\)
sub-aperture width, mm
\(K s\)
span of the virtual sources, mm
While the footprint limits, the width equals the focal width of one transmit and does not change with depth; once K s limits, it grows linearly with the distance from the focus. The estimate ignores bandwidth, the RTB weights and the focal-zone failure of the virtual-source model. It is calibrated for uniform transmit apodization and for \(N_F\) of about 2 or more (cone model of virtual sources: Nikolov & Jensen 2002). With Hann apodization the footprint weights truncate the Hann-shaped synthetic aperture and the width stays near 2 mm (1.94–1.99 mm at 55–65 mm against an estimate growing from 1.8 to 2.5 mm).
Model, numerics and what Phase 1 leaves out
  • Array: 128 point elements at 0.3 mm pitch (38.1 mm); transmit k uses the elements within ±A/2 of xk that exist, so outer transmits of a wide aperture lose elements at the array ends (the readout counts them). Transmit delays use the true c.
  • Field: \(P_k = S(f)\sum_n a_n e^{-j2\pi f(\tau_n + r_n/c)}/(4\pi r_n)\) with \(S(f) = e^{-(f-f_0)^2/2\sigma^2}\), \(\sigma = B f_0/2.355\), summed over 401 frequencies on \([\max(0, f_0 - 4\sigma),\, f_0 + 4\sigma]\) MHz: the spectrum is clipped at 0 Hz without renormalisation, so the pulse is analytic. The frequency step is alias-free when Δf times the largest arrival-time spread plus the pulse autocorrelation length is below 1; the readout reports this ratio (0.28 for the K = 41 preset; 151 instead of 801 frequencies change the gated RTB map by 4·10−6 of its maximum, so 401 is a wide margin).
  • Gated measure: RTB \(\lvert q(x,z,0)\rvert = \lvert\int Q\,df\rvert\); single transmit \(\lvert p_0(x,z,t_0)\rvert\) at the line's sample time \(t_0 = z/c\); map A uses the same rule for each physical beam: transmit k is sampled at the time its own line (x = xk) samples, z/c, across all x, and the K maps are overlaid by maximum or sum (from the full sub-aperture even where the array would truncate it). With K = 1 maps A and B are therefore the same quantity, and with the default row-peak scaling the same image. The option "envelope peak" shows instead each transmit's envelope maximum over time, what a hydrophone records; it is wider than the gated beam away from the focus because it counts every part of the wavefront whenever it passes. The sound-speed error enters only the delay differences between transmits: the offset \(z/c_a - z/c\), common to all transmits, only shifts the image in depth and is removed.
  • Virtual-source delay near the focus: at \(z = z_f\), (R.1) predicts \((z_f + \lvert x_k\rvert)/c_a\) for transmit k, but its nearly flat focal wavefront reaches the line at about \(z_f/c\); the error \(\lvert x_k\rvert/c\) switches sign across the focus and fades within a few millimetres of it. With uniform weights and the energy measure it shows as a broad slab at \(z_f\) (every transmit's focal spot counted, 12.4–12.7 mm over 29.5–30.5 mm); with the gated measure the misaligned off-axis spots fall outside the sample instant and are suppressed (0.84 mm), leaving a gain loss on axis. With the default footprint weights neither measure shows a slab (energy width 1.0 mm at \(z_f\)). The gated loss is −1.5 dB for the K = 41 preset (footprint weights, virtual-source delay) and −27 dB for Na = 48, zf = 40 mm, 2.5 MHz, 50 % bandwidth, Tukey weights, where about nine overlapping transmits are misaligned (−20 dB once a 3 % sound-speed error is added, because the error partly decorrelates the cancellation).
  • Validity: RTB assumes each transmit has a focus to synthesize around. With the Fresnel number \(N_F = A^2/(4\lambda_0 z_f)\) below about 1 (F# ≳ 4; Na ≤ 24 at zf = 30 mm, Na ≤ 32 at 55 mm) the page flags it: the synthetic beam is then wider than one transmit over most depths (Na = 16, zf = 30 mm: 3.6 against 2.6 mm at 10 mm), (R.3) does not apply, and (R.1b) with its large zR is worse than (R.1) below the focus. At the defaults NF = 7.0.
  • Grid: x = −9.9 … 9.9 mm in 0.15 mm steps, z = 5 … 65 mm in 0.5 mm steps. Transmits that share an element-offset pattern are lateral shifts of one template field, computed once per depth row.
  • Widths are measured on the displayed amplitude (gated envelope or √E) between the outermost −6 dB crossings, single-transmit widths on a ±15.9 mm grid. A transmit "covers" the line at depth z when its own pulse energy at x = 0 is within 6 dB of its maximum at that depth (energy footprint); the footprint weights, C3, the reference line in C2 and the strip all use this one definition.
  • Gains are on-axis and relative to the central transmit alone; the SNR gain assumes independent white noise of equal power in every transmit's data, so it is the signal gain minus 10 log10Σwk2.
  • What the steps show. (1) Once the K transmits cover the line, the synthetic aperture is limited by the beam footprint, so the width stays near the focal width of one transmit; beyond about 50 mm the footprint needs more than 41 transmits (C3) and the width grows as K s limits, as (R.3) predicts. (2) The virtual-source delay is off by |xk|/c at the focus, so the few overlapping transmits partly cancel. (3) With correct delays at zf, uniform weights put every transmit's focal spot inside the gate, side by side along x (a 12 mm span, not a sum at one point); weights must follow the overlap. (4) The default display scales every depth to its row peak, which shows beam width but hides brightness. On a global scale the coherent sum scales with the number of overlapping transmits, 3 at the focus and 41 far from it; dividing each depth by Σ wk over the covering transmits gives approximately the average transmit amplitude, so what remains is roughly the depth dependence of a single focused beam (brightest at zf, about 15 dB lower at 60 mm; the covering transmits are off-axis, which makes it differ from the on-axis single beam by up to about 2 dB). The coherence option divides by Σ wk|qk| on the line and reads 0 dB on axis whenever the transmits add in phase. (5) The virtual sources form a sampled synthetic aperture of pitch s: its grating lobe sits near x ≈ ±λ0|z − zf|/s (7.3 mm at 10 mm for s = 1.2 mm; the footprint weights pull it 10–25 % inward) and is suppressed while s ≲ λ0F# (0.69 mm at the defaults). Weak lobes are already visible from s ≈ 0.6–0.75 mm (−24 to −18 dB near 20 mm); they are strongest about 10 mm either side of the focus (s = 1.2 mm: −10 dB near ±3.3 mm at 20 and 40 mm, −16 dB at 10 mm). (6) Left-to-right firing turns motion into a linear delay ramp, 2nkd/c, which tilts the synthetic beam by sin θ = 2d/s about the focus (R.4); on the line the gain collapses because the beam moves away, while its width and peak hardly change. A 3 % sound-speed error in the delay correction costs little here (+0.02 mm width at 10 mm) because only the delay differences between transmits matter; the error of the physical transmit focusing, the receive path and depth registration are not modelled. The covering transmits differ by at most about 0.9 mm of path (at 10 mm); the footprint excludes the outer transmits near the focus, where the outermost of the 41 would reach 1.7 mm at 20 mm and 2.8 mm at 25 mm. (7) Energy integrates over all time, so it counts each transmit's focal spot even when its pulse misses the line's sample instant; this separates the two measures only with uniform weights and the virtual-source delay (Try step 7).
  • Field II cross-check: a MATLAB script for Field II (notes, not part of this page) computes the K = 41, s = 0.3 mm, zf 30 mm preset with 0.05 × 0.05 mm elements, small enough to act as the point sources assumed here (a second run uses 0.27 mm wide, 5 mm high elements without an elevation lens to show element directivity); when its results are added, a Solver control appears above to switch the maps.
  • Solver: own frequency-domain point-source code written for this page (not Field II or another package), run in the browser; validated against an independent Python solver that superposes closed-form pulses in the time domain, with no frequency grid, no shift-invariance shortcut and its own implementation of (R.1), (R.1b) and the weights.
  • Not modelled here: receive beamforming and the two-way point-spread function, harmonics, attenuation, element directivity, motion other than uniform axial displacement, and the effect of the sound-speed error on transmit focusing and on the receive path.
