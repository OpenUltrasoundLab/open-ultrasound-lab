Optimization & automatic differentiation

Sound-speed maps, aberration laws and QUS parameter fits are now computed by iterating on a cost: beamform with the current guess, score the image or the residual, differentiate, step. This module rebuilds the optimizer's side of that loop (step size, conditioning, Newton-type methods, Adam), the reason imaging costs trap optimizers (cycle skipping), and the derivative machinery (dual numbers, reverse mode, complex step, Wirtinger calculus) that differentiable beamforming runs on.

A joint fit of sound speed (≈ 1540 m/s, sensitivity per m/s) and an amplitude or attenuation term (order 1) mixes column norms that differ by \(10^3\), so \(\kappa(\mathbf{J}^{T}\mathbf{J}) \approx 10^6\): the MATLAB lab gets \(1.22 \times 10^6\), which would cost gradient descent about \(8 \times 10^6\) iterations. Pitfall: a small gradient is not a small error

\(\lVert\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}^*\rVert \le \lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\rVert/\mu\). With \(\kappa = 10^4\), stopping at \(\lVert\nabla f\rVert = 10^{-6} L\) can leave an error of \(10^{-2}\) in the flat direction. Stop on the relative change of the parameters you care about (m/s, dB/cm/MHz) and report it in those units.

14.2Line search, Newton, Gauss–Newton and Levenberg–Marquardt

\(\lambda \to 0\) recovers Gauss–Newton; \(\lambda \to \infty\) gives a short step along the (diagonally scaled) negative gradient. Divide \(\lambda\) by 10 after a successful step, multiply by 10 after a failed one (Levenberg 1944, Marquardt 1963). The MATLAB lab fits \(A\,e^{-(t-t_0)^2/2\sigma^2}\cos\!\left(2\pi f(t - t_0)\right)\) to a noisy 4.5 MHz echo (peak SNR 26 dB). The linearized standard deviation of \(t_0\) at the solution is 0.69 ns. From a start 100 ns off (more than a quarter period, 56 ns) scaled gradient descent, Gauss–Newton and LM all land at \(t_0\) = 2.106 µs with \(A < 0\): half a period late with the polarity flipped, a local minimum whose residual is 2.6 times the noise level. Small gradient steps from the same start happen to follow the valley back to the right answer: basins of attraction belong to the algorithm, not only to the cost.

14.3Curved valleys, stochastic gradients and Adam

The Rosenbrock function \(f = (1 - x)^2 + 100(y - x^2)^2\) is the standard caricature of a fit with strongly coupled parameters (delay and frequency, sound speed and depth). Its minimum at (1, 1) has a Hessian \(\begin{bmatrix} 802 & -400 \\ -400 & 200 \end{bmatrix}\) with \(\kappa \approx 2508\), and the valley floor curves, so no fixed linear rescaling straightens it. It is a sum of squares with \(\mathbf{r} = \left(10(y - x^2),\ 1 - x\right)\), so Gauss–Newton applies.

When the cost is an average over many samples (pixels, frames, training images), the gradient of a random subset is an unbiased but noisy estimate. With a constant step the iterates do not converge; they settle into a noise ball. For one mode of curvature \(\lambda\) and gradient-noise variance \(\sigma^2\):

\[ e_{k+1} = (1 - \alpha\lambda)\,e_k - \alpha\, n_k \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \mathrm{E}\!\left[e_\infty^{2}\right] = \frac{\alpha\sigma^{2}}{\lambda(2 - \alpha\lambda)} \approx \frac{\alpha\sigma^{2}}{2\lambda} \]
(14.10)

\[ m_k = \beta_1 m_{k-1} + (1-\beta_1)\,g_k, \qquad v_k = \beta_2 v_{k-1} + (1-\beta_2)\,g_k^{2}, \qquad x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta\,\frac{\hat m_k}{\sqrt{\hat v_k} + \epsilon} \]

14.4Convexity, and why oscillatory data skip cycles

A function is convex if \(f(\theta\mathbf{a} + (1-\theta)\mathbf{b}) \le \theta f(\mathbf{a}) + (1-\theta)f(\mathbf{b})\) for \(0 \le \theta \le 1\); then every local minimum is global and any descent method with a sane step finds it. Least squares with a linear model, Tikhonov, L1 and TV regularizers (Module 13) are convex. As soon as the unknown sits inside the phase of an oscillating signal (a delay, a sound speed, an aberration law), the cost is not.

In transmission tomography and full-waveform inversion the data are traces across a path of length \(L\). A starting model that predicts the travel time to within half a period, \(\lvert\Delta t\rvert = L\lvert\Delta c\rvert/c^2 < 1/(2f)\), stays in the basin: \(\lvert\Delta c\rvert/c < \lambda/(2L)\). Breast-scale USCT (\(L\) = 200 mm, \(c\) = 1500 m/s) at 0.5 MHz tolerates 11 m/s, at 1 MHz 5.6 m/s. That is why ultrasound FWI starts from a travel-time (bent-ray) tomogram and from the lowest usable frequency. Pulse-echo autofocus has its own version, in Section 14.5.

Pitfall: a free polarity halves the basin

If the model amplitude may change sign, \(A\cos(2\pi f(t - t_0))\) and \(-A\cos(2\pi f(t - t_0 - T/2))\) are the same curve: false minima appear every \(T/2\), and the basin shrinks to \(\pm T/4\). The MATLAB echo fit shows it: a 100 ns start (\(T/4\) = 56 ns) converges to \(t_0\) + 106 ns with \(A < 0\). Constrain the sign, or give the model a free carrier phase so that \(t_0\) is set by the envelope alone (the free-phase fit recovers \(t_0\) = 1.994 µs from the same start).

14.5Autofocus: sound speed by gradient ascent on a focus metric

Module 07 showed that coherence peaks at the true sound speed. Turning that into an estimator means choosing what to maximize, over which pixels, and how to climb. The beamformer is a differentiable function of the assumed speed \(\hat c\). 14.9How experts think about it

Near a solution every cost is a quadratic, so \(\kappa\) of the Hessian decides how first-order methods converge and scaling or curvature (Newton, Gauss–Newton) is the cure. Far from the solution the shape of the basin decides everything: oscillatory data (RF, phase, a fixed-depth pixel) cut the basin to half a period, and the remedy is a cost whose basin is wider (envelope, low frequency, time-tied ROI, compounding) used first, then the precise cost. Derivatives are exact or they are suspect: write them by hand or by AD, check them against central differences once, and for complex parameters step along \(-\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial z^*\).

14.11Interview and qualifying-exam questions

I1Your gradient-descent reconstruction converges painfully slowly. Diagnose it and give the rate. · screen

Slow linear convergence means a large condition number of the Hessian. With the optimal fixed step \(2/(L + \mu)\) the error contracts by \((\kappa - 1)/(\kappa + 1)\) per iteration, about \((\kappa/2)\ln(1/\varepsilon)\) iterations: 691 for \(\kappa\) = 100 and \(\varepsilon = 10^{-6}\). First check the scaling (parameters in mixed units inflate \(\kappa\) by orders of magnitude; Jacobi scaling fixed \(1.2 \times 10^6 \to 1.17\) in the lab), then add momentum or use conjugate gradients (\(\sqrt\kappa\) dependence), or switch to Gauss–Newton if it is a least-squares problem. Follow-up: why does momentum help? Per mode it is a second-order recursion; tuned, its roots have modulus \((\sqrt\kappa-1)/(\sqrt\kappa+1)\), so iterations scale with \(\sqrt\kappa\).

I2How do you choose the step size when you do not know L? · screen

Backtracking with the Armijo condition: try \(\alpha\) (or 2× the last accepted \(\alpha\)), halve until \(f(\mathbf{x} + \alpha\mathbf{d}) \le f(\mathbf{x}) + 10^{-4}\alpha\nabla f^{T}\mathbf{d}\). Alternatively estimate \(L\) by a few power iterations on Hessian–vector products and use \(1/L\). For least squares, Gauss–Newton or LM chooses the step from curvature and needs no \(\alpha\). Follow-up: what can go wrong? Too short steps (add the Wolfe curvature condition) and, on nonconvex costs, long trial steps that jump basins; cap the trial step at a physically meaningful size (40 m/s in the autofocus demo).

I3You fit a backscatter or attenuation model with 3–5 parameters. GD, Gauss–Newton, LM or Adam? · onsite

LM: it behaves like Gauss–Newton (quadratic convergence for small residuals, about 10 iterations in the lab's echo fit) near the answer and like scaled gradient descent far from it, and never accepts an uphill step. Solve the subproblem with QR or with the scaled normal equations at this size, and scale parameters. At convergence \(\sigma^2(\mathbf{J}^{T}\mathbf{J})^{-1}\) gives the CRLB-type error bars (0.69 ns on \(t_0\)). Adam would take thousands of iterations and give no covariance. Follow-up: when does Gauss–Newton degrade? Large residuals (model error) or strong nonlinearity make the neglected \(\sum r_i\nabla^2 r_i\) term matter; convergence becomes linear.

I4Explain cycle skipping and quantify it for a 5 MHz pulse. · onsite

The L2 misfit between an echo and a delayed model is \(2E - 2R(\tau)\); for an RF pulse \(R(\tau) \approx E\,e^{-\tau^2/4\sigma^2}\cos 2\pi f_0\tau\), so local minima appear near every period (194, 388 ns for \(f_0\) = 5 MHz, \(\sigma\) = 0.125 µs) and the basin of the true minimum ends near \(\pm T_0/2\) = ±97 ns. A start beyond that converges to the wrong cycle. In travel-time form the starting model must satisfy \(\lvert\Delta c\rvert/c < \lambda/(2L)\). Remedies: envelope or correlation-based costs, low frequencies, multiscale schedules, better starting models. Follow-up: why not just low-pass the data? The energy is not there: a 1.5 MHz Gaussian low-pass keeps −29 dB of a 5 MHz, 60 % pulse, so noise dominates; you must transmit low frequencies.

I5Design a gradient-based global sound-speed estimator for a linear array. · research

Acquire several plane-wave angles; beamform IQ data on an ROI fixed in two-way time (pixel depth \(\hat ct/2\)) so that \(\hat c\) only changes focusing; maximize the coherently compounded energy or a coherence metric over ROIs with bright, diffuse echoes; get \(dM/d\hat c\) exactly (chain rule through delays and the interpolator, or AD), check it once by central differences; climb with a line search and a box constraint, from a coarse sweep or several starts. Report bias (calibrate on phantoms: the demo shows +2 to +10 m/s geometric bias) and precision across ROIs. Follow-up: why not maximize the energy of a single plane-wave image, or the RF value at the target depth? The first is nearly invariant to focusing (receive migration preserves energy); the second cycle-skips every \(\hat c^2/(2zf_0) \approx\) 8 m/s.

I6Forward or reverse mode for a differentiable beamformer with a 100 × 100 slowness map and a scalar loss? What limits it? · onsite

Reverse mode: \(10^4\) inputs, 1 output, so one backward pass at a small constant multiple of the forward cost, against \(10^4\) forward-mode passes. The limit is memory: the tape stores delays, interpolation indices and weights per pixel, element and transmit; for 256 × 256 pixels, 128 elements and 11 angles that is \(9.2 \times 10^7\) values, 0.74 GB per float64 quantity. Use checkpointing (recompute delays on the backward pass), float32 and patch-wise losses. Follow-up: when is forward mode better? Few parameters (a single \(\hat c\), a handful of layer speeds): one dual-number pass per parameter, no tape.

I7What is the complex-step derivative, why can h be 10⁻²⁰, and when does it fail? · screen

\(f'(x) \approx \mathrm{Im}\,f(x + ih)/h\). The Taylor series gives \(\mathrm{Im}\,f(x + ih) = hf'(x) - h^3f'''/6 + \dots\), with no subtraction of nearly equal numbers, so round-off does not grow as \(h\) shrinks and \(h = 10^{-20}\) yields machine precision (\(2.2 \times 10^{-16}\) in the lab) against ~\(10^{-8}\) for forward differences. It fails for non-analytic operations (abs, conj, MATLAB's ', max/min branches) and for code that already uses complex numbers, such as an IQ beamformer. Follow-up: what would you use for the IQ beamformer? Dual numbers or reverse-mode AD, or the hand-derived chain rule, verified once by central differences.

I8Derive the gradient of ‖y − Aw‖² with respect to complex w, and say how you would test it. · research

\(\mathcal{L} = (\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{A}\mathbf{w})^{H}(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{A}\mathbf{w})\). Treating \(\mathbf{w}\) and \(\mathbf{w}^*\) as independent, \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\mathbf{w}^* = -\mathbf{A}^{H}(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{A}\mathbf{w})\); the steepest-descent update is \(\mathbf{w} \leftarrow \mathbf{w} + \mu\mathbf{A}^{H}(\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{A}\mathbf{w})\), which is the LMS/Landweber step. Test: perturb \(\mathrm{Re}\,w_k\) and \(\mathrm{Im}\,w_k\) separately by \(\pm h\); \(d\mathcal{L}/d\mathrm{Re} + i\,d\mathcal{L}/d\mathrm{Im}\) must equal \(2\,\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial w_k^*\) (agreement \(2 \times 10^{-10}\) in the lab). Follow-up: what happens with the conjugate direction? The imaginary parts of all steps flip sign; the lab's run diverges to \(10^{62}\).

I9Adam converged to a slightly different answer each run and never quite settles. Why? · onsite

With a constant learning rate \(\eta\), Adam moves each parameter by about \(\eta\) per step (\(\hat m/\sqrt{\hat v} \approx \mathrm{sign}\,g\)), so it hovers in a band of order \(\eta\) around the minimum; with stochastic gradients there is also a noise floor proportional to the step (\(\alpha\sigma^2/(2\lambda)\) for SGD). Decay \(\eta\), increase the batch, or finish with a deterministic second-order method on the full cost. Follow-up: is Adam scale invariant? To a diagonal rescaling of the gradient yes (though a step of η still means different physical changes in different parameter units), to rotations no: a rotated narrow valley still slows it (Rosenbrock: 3034 iterations).

I10You maximize coherent energy over element phases on speckle. The optimizer converges, the energy exceeds that of the true correction, but the estimated screen is off by 0.14 rad rms. Explain. · research

The cost is the sample coherent energy \(\mathbf{w}^{H}\hat{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{w}\) with \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\) from \(K\) = 64 realizations. Speckle has no unique focal point: \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\) is far from rank one (\(\lambda_1/\lambda_2 \approx 2.4\) in the demo, against about 80 with a point 10 dB above the speckle) and its top eigenvector fits the sampling fluctuations of \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\), so the optimizer finds a law that beats the true one on this data set yet differs from it. A point-like target (guide star) makes \(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\) nearly rank one: at 10 dB target-to-speckle ratio the error drops to about 0.02 rad. More independent realizations, isoplanatic averaging or the distortion-matrix approach (Module 06) also help. Follow-up: why remove tilt before scoring? A linear phase only steers the beam (Module 07); it is not identifiable from coherence.

14.13Self-check

Q1κ = 400, tolerance 10⁻⁶. How many iterations for GD with the optimal step, and for tuned heavy ball (rate only)?

GD: \(\ln(10^6)/\ln(401/399)\) = 2763. Heavy ball: \(\sqrt\kappa\) = 20, \(\ln(10^6)/\ln(21/19)\) = 138 (expect somewhat more from the double-root transient). Ratio \(\approx\sqrt\kappa\) = 20.

Q2You run GD with α = 2.05/L. What happens to the stiff mode after 50 steps?

Its factor is \(1 - 2.05 = -1.05\): it alternates sign and grows by \(1.05^{50}\) = 11.5. Any \(\alpha > 2/L\) diverges, however small the excess.

Q3At 3 MHz, what is the RF delay basin, and how far apart are the ripples of an RF-at-fixed-depth autofocus metric at 30 mm (ĉ = 1540 m/s)?

Basin \(\approx \pm T_0/2\) = ±167 ns. Ripple period \(\hat c^2/(2zf_0) = 1540^2/(2 \times 0.03 \times 3 \times 10^6)\) = 13.2 m/s. Lower frequency widens both, which is the multiscale idea.

Q4A transmission path of 200 mm, c = 1500 m/s, f = 0.5 MHz. What starting-model error in c avoids cycle skipping?

\(\lambda\) = 3 mm, \(L/\lambda\) = 66.7, so \(\lvert\Delta c\rvert/c < 1/(2 \times 66.7)\) = 0.75 %: \(\lvert\Delta c\rvert\) < 11.25 m/s. Equivalently \(\lvert\Delta t\rvert = L\Delta c/c^2 < T/2\) = 1 µs.

Q5The complex step applied to g(x) = x·|x − 1| at x = 1.5 returns what, and what is the true derivative?

abs(z − 1) returns the real modulus, so \(\mathrm{Im}\,g(x + ih)/h = \lvert x - 1\rvert\) = 0.5. The true derivative is \(2x - 1\) = 2. A dual number (or AD) with abs overloaded as \(\mathrm{sign}(a)\,\dot a\) returns 2.

Q6L(z) = |z − a|². Which step μ in z ← z − μ ∂L/∂z* lands on a in one step, and what does the conjugated update do?

\(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial z^* = z - a\), so \(\mu\) = 1 lands exactly on \(a\) (in real coordinates the gradient is \(2(z - a)\) and the Newton step is 1/2 of it). The conjugated update \(z \leftarrow z - (z - a)^*\) moves the imaginary part the wrong way: from \(z = a + i\) it goes to \(a + 2i\).

Q7Why does the total energy of a single plane-wave image fail as an autofocus metric while the compounded energy over five angles works?

Receive DAS over a region nearly preserves the channel energy whatever the focusing law, so \(\sum\lvert b\rvert^2\) is almost flat in \(\hat c\) (the demo's 1-angle curve peaks 60–100 m/s high). Summing angles coherently before squaring adds cross terms that are maximal only when the angle images align, which happens at the right \(\hat c\): the 5-angle energy peaks within 2–10 m/s of the truth.
