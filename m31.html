Part V · Module 31

ULM end to end (capstone)

Ultrasound localization microscopy turns a diffraction-limited scanner into a microscope by treating every isolated microbubble as a point source, localizing it far below the wavelength and accumulating hundreds of thousands of positions into a vascular map. Every stage is an estimator from an earlier module: the clutter filter is an SVD (Module 06), motion correction is delay estimation (Module 11), detection is matched filtering (Module 10), localization is bounded by the CRLB (Module 12), the bubbles are those of Module 28 and the beams those of Module 23. This capstone runs the whole chain on simulated data, quantifies what each stage loses, and ends with the metrics (saturation, FRC) and the frontiers (high concentration, 3D, transcranial, functional) where the field is moving.

You will be able to

  • Design a ULM acquisition from first numbers: frame rate, angles, displacement per frame, linking distance, block length for the SVD filter, concentration and acquisition time
  • Choose SVD cut-offs from the singular spectrum and the Doppler content of the singular vectors, and predict which slow bubbles the filter removes
  • Set a detection threshold from a false-alarm rate, localize with weighted average, Gaussian fit, radial symmetry or interpolation, and compare each with the CRLB \(\sigma_x = w_x/\sqrt{\mathrm{ENR}}\)
  • Link localizations into tracks with nearest neighbour, greedy or Hungarian assignment and a Kalman predictor, and predict the linking error from the density and displacement
  • Compute saturation curves and acquisition times per vessel size, measure resolution with Fourier ring correlation without fooling yourself, and separate localization precision from resolution
  • Place the frontiers on the same map: PALA benchmarking, learned localization at high concentration, 3D ULM with matrix and row–column arrays, transcranial ULM with aberration correction, and functional ULM

31.1The pipeline as a chain of estimators, and the acquisition budget

A ULM image is the histogram of a very long list of estimated positions. Each entry of that list survived seven decisions: the bubble was in the image plane during an acquisition, its echo was separated from tissue, it was detected, it was isolated enough to be localized, its position was estimated, it was linked to its neighbours in time, and the resulting track was long enough to be kept. Each decision has a rate (what fraction passes) and an error (what the survivors carry with them), and the final image inherits both. Module 28 drew this chain as a schematic (Figure 28.E); here it runs.

The acquisition sets the numbers that every later stage lives with. Plane or diverging waves at \(N_a\) angles are compounded into one frame (Module 23); the pulse repetition frequency is capped by the round trip to the deepest echo; the frame rate fixes how far a bubble moves between frames, and that displacement, compared with the distance between bubbles, decides whether tracking works:

\[ f_{\mathrm{PRF}} \le \frac{c}{2z_{\max}}, \qquad \mathrm{FR} = \frac{f_{\mathrm{PRF}}}{N_a}, \qquad \delta = \frac{v}{\mathrm{FR}}, \qquad v_{\max} = d_{\mathrm{link}}\,\mathrm{FR}, \qquad \mathrm{ENR} \propto N_a \]
(31.1)

At 30 mm depth \(f_{\mathrm{PRF}} \le\) 25.7 kHz, so 5 angles allow at most 5.1 kHz; most systems run near 1 kHz, limited by data transfer and beamforming. At 1 kHz a 1 mm/s capillary bubble moves 1 µm per frame and a 30 mm/s arteriole bubble 30 µm (λ/10). PALA's default gate of 2 pixels of λ at 15.6 MHz (about 200 µm) tracks up to about 200 mm/s at 1 kHz. Five angles raise the ENR by 7 dB and shrink the localization error by \(\sqrt5\).

Two second-order effects matter for fast bubbles. A bubble moving axially at 30 mm/s during the 0.8 ms that 5 angles take at 5 kHz advances by 24 µm, a phase of \(4\pi\delta/\lambda\) = 0.98 rad across the compounding window; the coherent sum loses only 0.5 dB, but the loss grows quickly past π. And the same phase rotation from frame to frame is exactly the Doppler signal (Module 29) that lets the SVD separate bubbles from tissue in Section 31.2: speed is both what the clutter filter needs and what the tracker fears.

31.2Clutter filtering and motion correction: what the SVD keeps, what motion adds

Tissue echoes are 30–60 dB stronger than a bubble and almost perfectly coherent from frame to frame; bubbles are weak, sparse and moving. Module 06 built the filter that exploits the difference: stack a block of \(N_t\) frames as the columns of the Casorati matrix \(\mathbf{S}\) (pixels × frames), take its SVD and discard the first \(r\) components, which carry the energetic, slowly varying tissue. Module 29 showed the same filter in ultrafast Doppler. In ULM it decides which bubbles can ever be localized:

\[ \mathbf{S} = \sum_{k=1}^{N_t} \sigma_k\,\mathbf{u}_k\mathbf{v}_k^{H}, \qquad \mathbf{S}_{\mathrm{b}} = \mathbf{S} - \sum_{k=1}^{r} \sigma_k\,\mathbf{u}_k\mathbf{v}_k^{H} = \mathbf{S}\left(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{V}_r\mathbf{V}_r^{H}\right), \qquad \bar f_k = \frac{\mathrm{FR}}{2\pi}\,\arg\sum_{t} w_k[t+1]\,w_k^{*}[t],\ \ \mathbf{w}_k = \mathbf{v}_k^{*} \]

Too few components and the residual clutter sets the noise estimate, so the detector sees nothing (r = 0–2). The right cut-off (r = 3, the clutter rank of this simulation) gives the best recall and almost no false alarms. Each component beyond it removes some bubble energy: slow bubbles lose their mean, which leaves a negative imprint of their path in every frame of the block; those imprints are detected as false alarms and bias the localizations of their neighbours. PALA's in vivo example script keeps components 5 onward (cut-off [5 N]) and states that it must be adapted to the clutter and SNR.

31.3Detection: matched filter, threshold and the false-alarm budget

After filtering, each frame holds a few dozen bubbles in white-ish noise. The optimal detector for a known pulse shape in white Gaussian noise is the matched filter (Module 10): correlate the frame with the PSF and look for peaks. Normalized cross-correlation (NCC) with the PSF, used by several groups, is the same correlation divided by the local energy, which makes the threshold independent of bubble brightness at the price of detecting noise peaks wherever the energy is low. With an unknown phase the statistic is the magnitude, and its noise-only distribution sets the threshold:

\[ y(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{\mathbf{p}} s_{\mathrm{b}}(\mathbf{p})\,g(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{x}), \qquad P_{\mathrm{FA}} = e^{-\eta^{2}/\sigma_y^{2}}, \qquad P_{\mathrm{D}} = Q_1\!\left(\sqrt{2\,\mathrm{ENR}},\ \sqrt{2\ln(1/P_{\mathrm{FA}})}\right), \qquad \bar N_{\mathrm{FA}} \approx P_{\mathrm{FA}}\,\frac{A_{\mathrm{FOV}}}{\mathrm{FWHM}_x\,\mathrm{FWHM}_z} \]

\(P_{\mathrm{FA}} = 10^{-5}\) needs \(\eta^{2}/\sigma_y^{2}\) = 11.5. Detection is then 50 % at an ENR of 10.4 dB, 82 % at 12 dB and 99.9 % at 14.8 dB: the transition is sharp, so the bubbles that matter are lost to merging and filtering, not to the threshold. The 4.9 × 4.9 mm field of Demo 31.1 holds 341 cells of λ × 0.75λ, so noise alone gives 0.003 false alarms per frame; the 0.26 measured in m31_ulm_pipeline.m come from residual clutter and the filter's ghosts.

Two practical detectors depart from the textbook. PALA's localization code keeps a fixed number of the brightest local maxima per frame (its numberOfParticles, 30–100) with a safeguard on the number of local maxima inside the FWHM mask: in effect a fixed-count detector whose false-alarm rate rises whenever the frame holds fewer bubbles than the count. Others threshold the NCC with the PSF. Either way the detector cannot separate two bubbles closer than about one FWHM: they produce one peak. In m31_ulm_pipeline.m only 35 % of the detected bubbles have no neighbour within 1.5 FWHM. This is the density trade-off of Module 28, \(P_{\mathrm{iso}} = e^{-nA}\): at most \(1/(eA)\) isolated bubbles per unit area per frame, 1.65 per mm² at 5 MHz.

m31_localization_methods.m runs all of them (plus cubic interpolation) on one bubble and compares with the bound. On λ/4 pixels with white pixel noise, at 25 dB: weighted average 2.86×, Gaussian 3-point 3.39×, cubic interpolation 4.14×, radial symmetry 1.76×, ML 0.98× the CRLB. On λ/2 pixels the Gaussian fit improves to 1.34× and radial symmetry to 1.19×, because three pixels then span most of the PSF. Two lessons follow. Fine pixels do not help a 3-point estimator: it uses three samples, and their noise is independent while the PSF is wide. And the weighted average floors at 11–15 µm at high ENR because it is biased toward the window centre (pixel locking: the sub-pixel part of its estimates has a standard deviation of 0.19 pixel instead of the 0.29 of a uniform distribution). The ML estimate reaches the bound from about 20 dB at every pixel size but costs a search per bubble; that cost, at millions of bubbles, is why simpler estimators persist. Note that beamformed noise is band-limited, not white; Module 12 showed that this narrows the gap between estimators.

Overlap. The bound assumes one bubble. Two equal bubbles at 30 dB each merge into a single peak in 63 % of cases at 0.5 FWHM separation and 40 % at 0.75 FWHM; at 1 FWHM they are detected separately but each estimate is pulled 44 µm toward its partner, more than ten times the bound; beyond 2 FWHM the bias vanishes (m31_localization_methods.m, part 2). This is the regime where learned and sparsity-based localizers earn their place (Section 31.7).

31.5Tracking: nearest neighbour, optimal assignment and a Kalman predictor

Linking localizations into tracks does three jobs at once. It rejects false alarms (noise does not move along vessels for 15 frames), it measures velocity, and it lets the renderer draw continuous paths instead of dots. Between two frames the problem is a bipartite matching: localizations \(\mathbf{x}_i\) of frame \(t\) against \(\mathbf{y}_j\) of frame \(t+1\). Nearest neighbour takes the closest candidate for each bubble independently; the Hungarian algorithm (Kuhn–Munkres) finds the one-to-one matching of minimal total cost; a predictor moves each \(\mathbf{x}_i\) to where its velocity says it should be before matching. For Poisson-distributed bubbles the crowding is captured by one number:

\[ \rho = n\,d^{2}, \qquad P_{\mathrm{c,NN}} = e^{-\pi\rho}, \qquad \min_{\pi} \sum_i C_{i\pi(i)}, \quad C_{ij} = \bigl\lVert \mathbf{y}_j - (\mathbf{x}_i + \hat{\mathbf{v}}_i\,\Delta t) \bigr\rVert^{2} \ \text{(gated)}, \qquad P_{\mathrm{c,pred}} \approx \frac{1}{1 + \pi\rho\, d_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}/d^{2}} \]

31.6Rendering, saturation and resolution: when is the image finished, and how sharp is it?

Rendering. Kept tracks are drawn on a grid of λ/10 to λ/20 (PALA renders at λ/10 by default; Hingot et al. 2019 used 5 µm pixels). Tracks are interpolated so that consecutive points are less than a render pixel apart, and each track adds one count per pixel it crosses. Three maps come out of the same tracks: density (tracks per pixel, displayed with a compressive scale because big vessels carry orders of magnitude more bubbles), velocity (mean track speed per pixel), and direction (the sign of the axial velocity, which separates arterioles from venules running side by side in the cortex). The density map is a histogram, and histograms have sampling problems.

Saturation. A pixel shows a vessel only after a bubble has crossed it. If bubbles cross a vessel of diameter \(d\) at the flux \(\eta\,C\,Q\) and each one paints one of the \(\max(d,l)/l\) pixel columns across it, every column is visited as a Poisson process. The time constant is the acquisition time of Hingot et al. (Sci. Rep. 2019, their Eq. 4: \(T_{\mathrm{acq}} = d/(C\,l\,Q)\)), generalized here with the efficiency \(\eta\) and with \(\max(d,l)\) for vessels thinner than a pixel:

\[ T(d) = \frac{\max(d,l)}{\eta\,C\,Q(d)\,l}, \qquad Q = \frac{\pi d^{2}}{4}\,v, \qquad S(t) = 1 - e^{-t/T}, \qquad T_{3\mathrm{D}}(d) = \frac{\max(d,l)^{2}}{\eta\,C\,Q(d)\,l^{2}} \]
(31.10)

Real \(\eta\) is far below 1, which is why Hingot et al. found that vessels below 25 µm may need more than 10 minutes. \(T\) scales as \(d^{-2}\) above the pixel size and \(d^{-3}\) below it (with \(v \propto d\)); in 3D the extra factor \(d/l\) makes small-voxel volumes much slower.

A Monte Carlo of the 20 µm vessel (four columns of 5 µm) reproduces \(S(T)\) = 0.634 against 0.632. When bubbles follow a parabolic flow profile, the wall columns are visited less often and the vessel fills more slowly (0.862 instead of 0.900 at 2.3 T): images look narrower than the lumen until they saturate, which biases diameter measurements early in an acquisition.

How the halves are split matters. Localizations of one track lie on one streamline, a sub-resolution structure that the other half does not share unless the split puts points of the same track on both sides. m31_saturation_frc.m (40 vessels of 10–80 µm, σ = 5 µm, 1/7 threshold): splitting by track gives 64 µm with 300 tracks, 49 µm with 1000 and 36 µm with 10,000; splitting by localization reports 28–31 µm from the start. The track split is honest about undersampling; the localization split measures the precision along streamlines and ignores the sampling term. At σ = 20 µm both converge (about 60 µm), because precision then dominates. At low counts the half-bit curve is the most conservative of the three thresholds (88 µm against 64 µm for 1/7 with 300 tracks).

31.7Frontiers: validation, high concentration, 3D, through the skull, and function

Validation. Every number in this module was measured against a ground truth that only simulation provides. The field adopted the same discipline with PALA (Heiles et al., Nature Biomed. Eng. 2022): seven localization algorithms scored with 18 metrics (localization errors, true and false positives, Jaccard index, processing time, gridding index, saturation) combined into a global score, on two in silico datasets (isolated PSFs; a simulated microvascular flow) and an in vivo rat brain, with three supplementary in vivo datasets (rat brain bolus, rat kidney, mouse tumour), code and data released. The lesson for anyone proposing a new localizer, tracker or filter: report it on shared data, with the same metrics, against the same baselines, and separate detection (how many) from precision (how well).

High concentration and learned localization. The density optimum \(n^{*} = 1/A\) of Section 31.3 caps the useful localizations per frame, and with them the acquisition time of (31.10). Any method that localizes overlapping bubbles shrinks the effective exclusion area \(A\). Deep-ULM (van Sloun et al., IEEE Trans. Med. Imaging 2021) trains a convolutional network on simulated frames with densities up to 260 bubbles per cm² to output positions on an 8× finer grid, and reports in vivo vessel widths of about 20–30 µm in a rat spinal cord, with centroid-based ULM breaking down as density rises (its supplementary density sweep). Sparsity-based recovery (for example SUSHI, one of the comparisons in Deep-ULM's supplementary figures) does the same with a model instead of training. Both inherit a forward model: the network its simulator (PSF, noise, clutter residue), the sparse solver its dictionary. Domain gap is to learned ULM what model error is to sound-speed tomography (Module 32).

3D. A 2D image plane integrates bubbles over the elevation slice (0.5–1 mm), so vessels that cross the slice appear as dots and the in-plane density includes bubbles from out of plane. Volumetric ULM removes both problems at a cost: a 32 × 32 matrix array (1024 elements; Heiles et al., IEEE Trans. Med. Imaging 2019) needs 1024 channels or multiplexing, while a row–column array addresses an \(N \times N\) aperture with \(2N\) channels (Jensen et al., IEEE TUFFC 2020: 62 + 62 channels at 3 MHz, localization precision of about 20 µm laterally and 9 µm axially on a point phantom), with weaker focusing and edge waves (Module 19). Saturation is slower in 3D by the factor \(\max(d,l)/l\) of (31.10), but tracks no longer leave the plane, so η rises.

Through the skull. Transcranial ULM in patients (Demené et al., Nature Biomed. Eng. 2021) imaged deep brain vessels in adults at resolutions of up to 25 µm, using adaptive speckle tracking to correct both micrometric brain motion and the aberrations of transcranial propagation; it separated systolic from diastolic flow and showed vortices in a small deep aneurysm. The skull adds a phase screen (Module 33). Its defocusing part widens the PSF and costs precision as \(1/\sqrt{\mathrm{ENR}}\); its linear part steers the beam and moves every bubble behind it:

Function. Functional ULM (Renaudin et al., Nature Methods 2022) turns the map into a movie: in rats it reached 6.5 µm spatial and 1 s temporal resolution by accumulating localizations in 5 s sliding windows (1 s step) over 20 repeated stimulations (100 s of data per image) at 1000 frames/s, and found activation down to subcortical nuclei, with the largest relative flow increase (+49 %) in intraparenchymal vessels of the barrel cortex. 31.11Interview and qualifying-exam questions

I1Walk me through a ULM pipeline and name the number that limits each stage. · screen

Acquire compounded plane waves (ENR per frame and displacement per frame, \(\delta = v/\mathrm{FR}\)); SVD clutter filter (slowest bubble kept, about \(\lambda/(2T_b)\) axially); motion correction (residual adds in quadrature); detection (false-alarm rate and merging below one FWHM); localization (\(w/\sqrt{\mathrm{ENR}}\) plus estimator bias); tracking (\(e^{-\pi n d^{2}}\), gate, minimum length); rendering (saturation \(T = \max(d,l)/(\eta CQl)\), FRC). Follow-up: which stage would you improve first for capillary maps? Acquisition time, through η: more isolated bubbles per frame (overlap-tolerant localization) and fewer slow bubbles lost in the filter.

I2How do you choose the SVD cut-off, and what goes wrong on each side? · screen

Look for the drop in the singular spectrum, the jump in the mean Doppler of the temporal vectors (tissue at a few hertz, bubbles at tens), and spatial vectors that stop looking like the B-mode (Baranger et al. 2018 automate this). Too low: residual clutter dominates the noise estimate and the detector finds nothing or fires on tissue. Too high: slow bubbles are removed, and their removed mean leaves negative imprints that become false alarms and bias neighbours (m31_ulm_pipeline.m: r = 3 gives 0.26 false alarms per frame, r = 4 gives 2.45). Follow-up: what if tissue moves a lot? The tissue rank grows; use shorter or block-wise SVD, or register before filtering.

I3Derive the localization CRLB for a Gaussian PSF and give numbers at 5 MHz. · onsite

For \(s = a\,g(x - x_0)\) in complex white noise of density \(N_0\), the Fisher information on \(x_0\) is \((2/N_0)\lvert a\rvert^{2}\int\!\!\int (\partial_x g)^{2}\); with \(g = e^{-x^{2}/2w_x^{2} - z^{2}/2w_z^{2}}\) that is \(\mathrm{ENR}/w_x^{2}\) with \(\mathrm{ENR} = \lvert a\rvert^{2}\pi w_x w_z/N_0\), and the unknown phase costs nothing because \(\partial_{x_0}s\) is orthogonal to \(s\). So \(\sigma_x \ge w_x/\sqrt{\mathrm{ENR}}\): 13 µm at 20 dB, 4 µm at 30 dB (\(w_x\) = 131 µm). Follow-up: why do real localizers miss it? Few-sample estimators, pixel locking, overlap, and the threshold region below about 15 dB.

I4Your Gaussian-fit localizer runs at three times the CRLB. Is the CRLB wrong? · onsite

Probably not: a 3-point fit on λ/4 pixels uses three samples of a PSF four pixels wide, with independent noise in each. m31_localization_methods.m gives 3.4× at 25 dB for white pixel noise, 1.3× at λ/2, while the ML estimate (normalized matched-filter maximum) reaches 0.98× and radial symmetry 1.8×. Check that it is not overlap (split errors by nearest-neighbour distance) and not bias (sub-pixel histogram). Follow-up: is ML affordable? Separable templates and a two-stage grid make it a few thousand multiply-adds per bubble.

I5Nearest neighbour or Hungarian for ULM tracking? · onsite

Depends on \(\rho = n d^{2}\). NN is right with probability \(e^{-\pi\rho}\); at 1 kHz, 5 localizations per mm² and 10 mm/s, ρ = 5×10⁻⁴ and every method is above 99.8 %. Hungarian matters at low frame rates, fast flow or high density: at ρ = 0.2 NN gets 52 %, Hungarian 72 %, Hungarian with a constant-velocity prediction 96 % (m31_tracking_hungarian.m); greedy global assignment is worse than NN (42 %). Follow-up: what breaks links in practice then? Missed detections, merged bubbles and false alarms: gap closing and a Kalman gate help more than the assignment rule.

I6How long must I acquire to map 10 µm vessels with 5 µm pixels? · onsite

\(T = \max(d,l)/(\eta C Q l)\) (Hingot et al. 2019). With \(C\) = 200 /µL, \(v\) = 2 mm/s: \(Q\) = 1.57×10⁻⁴ µL/s, flux 0.031 bubbles/s, two columns, so \(T\) = 64 s at η = 1 and 90 % after 2.3 T = 2.4 min; with η = 0.1 about 25 minutes. Follow-up: does a faster frame rate help? No; flux sets new bubbles per second. Higher concentration helps until overlap caps η (Section 31.3).

I7A paper claims "λ/10 resolution." What do you ask? · research

Is it precision (from repeated localizations or track smoothness) or resolution (separation of two vessels, or FRC)? If FRC: split by tracks or by localizations, which threshold (half-bit, 2σ, 1/7), how many tracks, and was the image saturated? m31_saturation_frc.m shows the localization split reporting 28–31 µm when the track split gives 64 µm with 300 tracks. Then: motion correction residual, aberration (bias, not blur), and whether vessels below the saturation diameter are missing rather than resolved. Follow-up: one number you would add? The saturation curve.

I8Why does uncorrected motion matter more than localization precision in vivo? · research

Errors add in quadrature: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2} = \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2} + \sigma_{\mathrm{m}}^{2} + \sigma_{\mathrm{reg}}^{2}\). A breathing excursion of 100 µm is 71 µm rms against 5–15 µm of localization error, so a vessel appears 170 µm wide. Correction by speckle registration of the tissue signal per block reduces it to the registration error (a few micrometres at good tissue SNR), but out-of-plane motion and deformation remain. Motion along a vessel is harmless; across it is fatal. Follow-up: how would you validate? Measure the spread across a vessel of known size: \(s^{2} = R^{2}/5 + \sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}\) for a 2D parabolic profile counted once per bubble (\(R^{2}/6\) to \(R^{2}/4\) for a cylinder, depending on how localizations are counted).

I9What changes in transcranial ULM, and why is aberration worse than blur? · research

Lower frequency (larger PSF, so worse \(w/\sqrt{\mathrm{ENR}}\)), skull attenuation (lower ENR), longer acquisitions, and a phase screen. The quadratic part of the screen defocuses (precision); the linear part steers the beam and displaces every bubble behind it by \(z c \alpha\): 77 µm for 10 ns across 10 mm at 50 mm. A bias does not average out, so the map is warped with full confidence. Demené et al. (2021) corrected aberration and brain motion with adaptive speckle tracking. Follow-up: how would you estimate the screen? From the bubbles themselves as guide stars, or from the distortion matrix (Module 33).

I10Make the case for, and against, deep-learning localization at high concentration. · research

For: the density cap \(1/(eA)\) limits isolated bubbles per frame; a localizer that separates bubbles at half an FWHM raises the cap fourfold and cuts acquisition time fourfold. Deep-ULM trained on densities up to 260 /cm² and reports 20–30 µm in vivo where centroid methods break down. Against: it inherits its simulator (PSF shape, clutter residue, noise), errors become domain gap, and it is hard to give a per-bubble uncertainty. I would ask for PALA-style benchmarks on shared data, recall and precision separately, and a test on a PSF it was not trained on. Follow-up: a physics-anchored alternative? Sparse recovery or unrolled networks with the beamformer as forward model.

I11Design a 3D ULM system: matrix or row–column array? · research

Matrix (e.g. 32 × 32 = 1024 elements, Heiles et al. 2019): good focusing in both directions, but 1024 channels or multiplexing, small aperture per channel budget, lower ENR per element. Row–column (Jensen et al. 2020: 62 + 62 channels): \(2N\) channels for an \(N \times N\) aperture, but one-way focusing in each direction, edge waves and lower contrast, so stronger clutter filtering and careful apodization. Either way saturation is slower by \(\max(d,l)/l\), volume rates are lower (larger ρ, so assignment and prediction matter), and the data rate is the system bottleneck. Follow-up: what does 3D fix? Out-of-plane bubbles and vessels, so tracks are longer and η rises.
