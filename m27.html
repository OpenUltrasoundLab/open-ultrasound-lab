Spectral estimation & quantitative ultrasound

Every QUS number (attenuation coefficient, backscatter coefficient, effective scatterer diameter, spectral slope) is a function of an estimated power spectrum of a short, speckled RF gate. This module rebuilds the estimators, states their bias and variance in closed form, and carries the units (Np vs dB, amplitude vs power, one-way vs two-way) through every step to the attenuation and size estimates.

You will be able to

  • Predict the bias (window convolution) and variance (\(\chi^2_2\), 5.57 dB per bin) of a periodogram, and what Welch averaging, multitapers and AR models change
  • Attach a degrees-of-freedom count and a \(\chi^2\) confidence interval to any averaged spectrum
  • Derive the \(-4\alpha(f)\Delta z\) log spectral ratio (power, two-way, \(\alpha\) in Np) and convert it to dB/cm/MHz without a factor-2 or 8.686 error
  • Choose between the spectral log difference, the reference phantom regression and the centroid downshift, and predict each one's bias sources and standard deviation from \(K\), \(B\), \(T\) and the ROI length
  • Fit a Gaussian form factor for the effective scatterer diameter, know the \(ka\) range where it is identifiable, and see how an attenuation error leaks into it

Ultrasound link

The spectrum of one RF gate is itself speckle, in frequency. A 4 mm gate at 5 MHz gives a spectrum whose every bin has 100 % standard deviation. QUS gets its averaging from \(K\) laterally independent A-lines (separated by at least a beam width) and from the bandwidth, never from the gate alone. When a paper reports a spectrum, ask how many independent looks went into it.

Pitfall

Zero padding (Module 04) interpolates the same noisy spectrum onto a finer grid; smoothing adjacent padded bins averages correlated values and reduces variance far less than the number of bins suggests. Count independent bins at spacing ≈ ENBW, not FFT bins.

27.2Averaging: Bartlett, Welch and honest error bars

\[ \frac{\mathrm{var}\{\hat S\}}{S^2} \times \Delta f_{\mathrm{res}} \approx \frac{1}{K} \times \frac{K f_s}{N} = \frac{f_s}{N} = \frac{1}{T} \]

Intuition

Two echoes separated by \(\tau\) multiply the spectrum by \(\lvert 1 + a\, e^{-j2\pi f\tau}\rvert^2\), a cosine ripple with period \(1/\tau\). This is the spectral signature of quasi-periodic scatterer spacing (mean scatterer spacing estimation looks for exactly this ripple). The estimator can only see it if its segment, or gate, is longer than \(\tau\), which ties spectral resolution directly to the axial extent of the gate.

27.3Multitaper spectra: Slepian sequences

Pitfall

Multitaper is not a free lunch for a smooth speckle spectrum: for the same spectral-window bandwidth, Hann–Welch and multitaper give nearly the same variance (m27_multitaper_dpss.m: variance × bandwidth × \(T\) = 1.36 vs 1.39). Its real advantages are leakage control over a large dynamic range (with low-order tapers or adaptive weighting), use of the full gate, and a principled bandwidth knob. Every extra high-order taper buys variance with leakage, and beyond \(K = 2NW - 1\) the price is steep.

Intuition

For a smooth speckle spectrum, what matters is low-order structure, so AR helps the spectral moments more than the resolution. In m27_ar_burg.m the centroid of a 64-sample speckle gate has std 0.36 MHz from a Hann periodogram and 0.29 MHz from Burg at any order from 4 to 16. The reason: the one-sided centroid equals \(f_s/4 - (2f_s/\pi^2) \sum_{\text{odd } l} \rho[l]/l^2\), dominated by lag 1, and every Burg model of order \(\ge l\) reproduces the same \(\rho[0..l]\).

Pitfall

AR peak heights are not powers (the area under the peak is), Burg peaks shift and split with initial phase at high SNR, and an AR spectrum has no \(\chi^2\) error bar. Use AR for locating features and for moments on very short gates; use averaged Fourier spectra when you need calibrated power, as in BSC estimation.

27.12Interview and qualifying-exam questions

I1Why doesn't a longer gate make the periodogram less noisy, and what does? · screen

Each DFT bin of Gaussian data is circular complex Gaussian, so \(\lvert X\rvert^2\) is exponential with std equal to its mean. A longer record adds bins at spacing \(1/T\); it does not average within a bin. Variance falls only by averaging independent looks: segments (Bartlett/Welch), tapers (multitaper), adjacent bins (smoothing), or in QUS independent A-lines. Each costs resolution or spatial extent. Follow-up: "how many independent looks does a Welch estimate with Hann 50 % overlap have?" \(K_{\mathrm{eff}}\) from the overlap formula (27.7), e.g. 14.3 for 15 segments.

I2Derive the factor in the log spectral ratio and convert a measured slope to dB/cm/MHz. · screen

Amplitude decays as \(e^{-\alpha_0 f z}\) one way (\(\alpha_0\) in Np/cm/MHz); the echo travels \(2z\), and power squares it: \(e^{-4\alpha_0 f z}\). Between gates \(\Delta z\) apart, \(\ln S(z_2) - \ln S(z_1) = -4\alpha_0 f\Delta z\). If that difference has slope −0.46 per MHz for \(\Delta z\) = 2 cm: \(\alpha_0\) = 0.46/8 = 0.0575 Np/cm/MHz = 0.50 dB/cm/MHz. Follow-up: "and with amplitude spectra?" The slope is −0.23 per MHz and you divide by \(2\Delta z\): same answer.

I3What does a reference phantom cancel, and what does it not? · onsite

It cancels the transducer response, focusing/diffraction, gain, TGC and gate/window factors, because they multiply both spectra identically at the same depth (27.17). It does not cancel a sound-speed mismatch (the focus and beam shape move), differences in overlying attenuation (critical for BSC, irrelevant to the depth slope inside a homogeneous ROI), errors in the phantom's own \(\alpha_r\) and \(\mathrm{BSC}_r\) (they pass straight into the result), nonlinear propagation differences at high pressure, or electronic noise. Follow-up: "how would you check?" Scan a second phantom of known properties as a sample, and check the ln ratio for curvature in depth.

I4You need σ(α) ≤ 0.05 dB/cm/MHz with a 3–7 MHz band. Size the ROI. · onsite

Use \(\sigma \approx \sqrt{4.5\gamma\psi_1(K)c/(B^3L^3)}\) in Np (27.26), Hann \(\gamma = 1.75\), \(c\) = 0.154 cm/µs, \(B\) = 4 MHz. Target 0.05/8.686 = 0.00576 Np. With \(K = 32\) (\(\psi_1\) = 0.0317): \(L^3\) = 4.5·1.75·0.0317·0.154/(64·0.00576²) ≈ 18.2 → \(L\) ≈ 2.6 cm. With \(K = 16\), \(L\) ≈ 3.3 cm. Lines must be independent, i.e. spaced by about a beam width (\(\lambda F_\#\) ≈ 0.6 mm at 5 MHz, F/2): 32 independent lines span about 2 cm laterally; denser lines are correlated and count for less than their number. Follow-up: "what if the band shrinks to 2 MHz at depth?" \(\sigma\) grows by \(2^{1.5}\) = 2.8×, so \(L\) must grow by 2.

I5A 2 mm gate: Welch, multitaper or AR? · onsite

A 2 mm gate is about 104 samples at 40 MHz, so Fourier resolution is ≈ 0.4 MHz. For calibrated power (BSC, SLD) use multitaper (\(NW\) ≈ 3–4, \(K\) = 5–7): full use of the gate, \(\nu \approx 2K\), controlled leakage; Welch would split an already short gate. For locating features below \(1/T\) or for moments, Burg AR with AIC-chosen order; never use AR peak heights as power. In all cases the dominant averaging still comes from lateral lines. Follow-up: "what does \(NW\) cost?" Spectral smoothing over \(2W = 2NW f_s/N\).

I6Centroid downshift or spectral log difference? · onsite

\(\partial f_c/\partial z = -4\alpha_0\sigma^2(z)\) holds exactly (27.18), so the centroid needs the spectral variance at every depth; using the transmit \(\sigma_p^2\) assumes a Gaussian, unchanged by BSC, diffraction and noise. The log-ratio needs a reference but no shape model. On the same data both reached 0.030 dB/cm/MHz std; the centroid kept a 6 % bias. Choose the centroid when no reference exists and the shape is stable (or estimate \(\sigma^2\) per depth), the log ratio otherwise. Follow-up: "how does the BSC frequency dependence affect each?" It cancels in the SLD depth slope, but changes \(\sigma^2\) and biases the centroid with a fixed \(\sigma_p^2\).

I7Why does electronic noise bias attenuation estimates low, and how do you guard against it? · research

Noise adds a roughly flat power floor. Deep gates and high frequencies, where the signal is weakest, are lifted the most, so the distal spectrum looks less attenuated and the ln-ratio slope shrinks. Guards: measure the noise spectrum (no transmit) and subtract it in power before taking logs; restrict the band per depth to SNR above ~10 dB; weight the regression by the estimated variance; report the usable depth. Follow-up: "does noise subtraction bias anything?" It can produce negative powers at low SNR; clip or drop those bins, which reintroduces a small bias.

I8When can you estimate an effective scatterer diameter, and what correlates with it? · research

When the band covers \(ka\) of roughly 0.5 to 1.5, where the Gaussian form factor bends the \(f^4\) trend (local exponent \(4 - 1.654(ka)^2\), (27.25)). Below \(ka\) ≈ 0.3 the fit is noise (36 % failures for 20 µm at 3–7 MHz). The estimate correlates with the attenuation compensation (an error of 0.1 dB/cm/MHz at 3 cm takes 100 µm to 78 µm) and with the assumed form factor. Follow-up: "how would you pick the transducer?" Put the band so \(ka\) spans the size range of interest, then check SNR at the needed depth.

I9How would you turn SLD into an attenuation image with better resolution? · research

Write the log-spectral data from many small gates as a linear model \(y = A\alpha + b + \varepsilon\), with \(\alpha\) a spatial map (and optionally BSC-ratio terms as nuisance parameters), and solve it with regularization: Tikhonov or total variation in space, the same machinery as Module 13. Regularized SLD (Coila & Lavarello, IEEE TUFFC, 2018) is one published example. Report bias and resolution of the regularized map (model-resolution matrix), not just its smoothness. Follow-up: "what is the price?" Bias toward the prior: TV shrinks contrast of small inclusions, Tikhonov blurs edges.

I10Why might a log-averaged spectrum differ systematically between two depths even with no attenuation difference? · onsite

The expected log of an average of \(K\) exponentials, \(\psi(K) - \ln K\), depends on the effective number of independent looks. If the distal lines are more correlated (beam widens past focus) or contain noise, \(K_{\mathrm{eff}}\) differs between depths and the offsets no longer cancel: −2.51 dB at \(K = 1\) vs −0.07 dB at \(K = 32\). Fix by averaging power before the log, matching \(K_{\mathrm{eff}}\), or correcting with \(\psi(K_{\mathrm{eff}})\). Follow-up: "average log or log average?" Log of the average: lower variance and a bias that vanishes as \(1/(2K)\).

I11How would you validate a new attenuation estimator before a 510(k) or a paper? · research

Phantoms with \(\alpha\) measured independently by insertion loss (narrowband substitution) over the same band; several \(\alpha\) values and depths; bias and standard deviation versus ROI size, not only means; a layered phantom to test overlying-layer immunity; a sound-speed mismatch case; and in vivo repeatability (same subject, repeated scans, different operators). Report units, band, ROI dimensions and the number of independent lines. Public benchmarks that pair attenuation with sound-speed ground truth help comparison across methods. Follow-up: "what accuracy is enough?" Set it from the clinical decision threshold and the population spread, then size the ROI with the precision formula.

27.14Self-check

Q1A single Hann-windowed periodogram of a 256-sample speckle gate: what are the mean and standard deviation of \(10\log_{10}(P/S)\) in band?

−2.51 dB and 5.57 dB (27.5), independent of \(N\) and of the window (the window changes the bias, not the \(\chi^2_2\) distribution).

Q2Welch with N = 1024, M = 128, Hann, 50 % overlap: how many segments, how many effective looks, and how wide is the 95 % interval?

\(N_b = 15\); \(K_{\mathrm{eff}} = 15/(1 + 2\cdot(14/15)\cdot(1/6)^2) = 14.3\); \(\nu = 28.5\); interval −1.99 to +2.60 dB around \(\hat S\), a 4.6 dB span.

Q3\(\ln S(z_2) - \ln S(z_1)\) has slope −0.46 per MHz for gates 2 cm apart (power spectra). What is \(\alpha_0\)? What if these were amplitude spectra?

Power: \(\alpha_0 = 0.46/(4\cdot 2)\) = 0.0575 Np/cm/MHz = 0.50 dB/cm/MHz. Amplitude: \(\alpha_0 = 0.46/(2\cdot 2)\) = 0.115 Np/cm/MHz = 1.0 dB/cm/MHz (the same data would have shown slope −0.23 in amplitude).

Q4N = 128 at 40 MHz with NW = 4: how many tapers, what resolution, what variance reduction?

\(K = 2NW - 1 = 7\) tapers, resolution \(2W = 2NW f_s/N\) = 2.5 MHz, variance about 1/7 of a single periodogram (0.17 measured on speckle because the spectrum is not flat over 2.5 MHz).

Q5A 5 MHz, 60 % Gaussian pulse shows a centroid slope of −0.187 MHz/cm. What attenuation does it imply, and what must you assume?

\(\sigma_p^2 = \sigma_f^2/2 = 1.274^2/2\) = 0.811 MHz²; \(\alpha_0 = 0.187/(4\cdot 0.811)\) = 0.0576 Np/cm/MHz = 0.50 dB/cm/MHz. Assumes a Gaussian power spectrum unchanged in shape by BSC, diffraction and noise, and \(\alpha \propto f\).

Q6Can you estimate an ESD of 20 µm with a 3–7 MHz probe? Of 150 µm?

20 µm: \(ka\) = 0.12–0.29, local exponent ≈ 3.9: no, the form factor is flat within the noise. 150 µm: \(ka\) = 0.92–2.14, exponent at 5 MHz ≈ 0.1: yes, well identified (149 ± 4 µm in the script).

Q7You double the ROI length of an attenuation estimate from 1.5 to 3 cm and halve the number of lines. Does precision improve?

\(\sigma \propto K^{-1/2}L^{-3/2}\): factor \(\sqrt{2}\) worse from \(K\), \(2^{1.5}\) = 2.83 better from \(L\), net 2.0× better.
