1Conventions used on this sheet

\[ X(f) = \int x(t)\, e^{-j2\pi f t}\, dt \qquad x(t) = \int X(f)\, e^{+j2\pi f t}\, df \]
Ordinary frequency f in Hz, so no 2π factors in front. With angular frequency ω the inverse gains 1/(2π). \(\operatorname{sinc}(x) = \sin(\pi x)/(\pi x)\), so sinc has zeros at every nonzero integer. \(\operatorname{rect}(t) = 1\) for \(\lvert t\rvert < \tfrac12\). Decibels: amplitude ratios use \(20\log_{10}\), power ratios use \(10\log_{10}\). Equation numbers such as (04.3) refer to the module and equation in the course.

3The comb, derived in four lines

Module 04, Section 04.1

\[ \operatorname{III}_T(t)\ \text{has period } T:\qquad c_k = \frac{1}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} \delta(t)\, e^{-j2\pi k t/T}\, dt = \frac{1}{T} \]
\[ \operatorname{III}_T(t) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_k e^{\,j2\pi k t/T}, \qquad e^{\,j2\pi k t/T} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \delta\!\left(f - \frac{k}{T}\right) \]
\[ \Rightarrow\quad \mathcal{F}\{\operatorname{III}_T\}(f) = \frac{1}{T}\operatorname{III}_{1/T}(f) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_k \delta\!\left(f - \frac{k}{T}\right) \]
A comb with spacing T transforms to a comb with spacing 1/T and weight 1/T. Narrow spacing in one domain means wide spacing in the other.
Sampling

Sampled signal \(x_s(t) = x(t)\operatorname{III}_T(t)\) ⇒ \(X_s(f) = X(f) \ast \tfrac{1}{T}\operatorname{III}_{1/T} = f_s\sum_k X(f - k f_s)\). The spectrum repeats every \(f_s\). Replicas overlap (alias) unless \(f_s > 2f_{\max}\).

Arrays

An array is a continuous aperture multiplied by a comb at pitch d. Its beam is the continuous beam convolved with a comb in \(\sin\theta\) at spacing λ/d. Those replicas are the grating lobes: \(\sin\theta_g = \sin\theta_0 \pm m\lambda/d\). Finite length turns each delta into a Dirichlet lobe of width ≈ λ/(Nd).

Pulsed Doppler

Slow-time sampling at the PRF is a comb in time, so the Doppler spectrum repeats every PRF. Velocities with \(\lvert f_D\rvert > \mathrm{PRF}/2\) wrap around: \(v_{\mathrm{Nyq}} = c\cdot\mathrm{PRF}/(4f_0\cos\theta)\).

Periodic ↔ discrete

Periodic in one domain means discrete (line spectrum) in the other. Sampled in time means periodic in frequency. That is why the DFT is both discrete and periodic in both domains.

5DFT, FFT and windows

Module 02 (derivations), Module 04 (resolution, window choice)

\[ X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\, e^{-j2\pi kn/N} \qquad x[n] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k} X[k]\, e^{j2\pi kn/N} \qquad \sum_n \lvert x[n]\rvert^2 = \frac{1}{N}\sum_k \lvert X[k]\rvert^2 \]
Bin spacing \(\Delta f = f_s/N\). True resolution ≈ \(1/T_{\mathrm{obs}} = f_s/N\). Zero padding interpolates the spectrum but adds no resolution. Multiplying DFTs gives circular convolution, so pad to \(N \ge L + M - 1\) for linear convolution. The FFT costs about \(N\log_2 N\) operations instead of \(N^2\), and fftshift puts 0 Hz in the middle.
