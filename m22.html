Acoustic output, bioeffects and regulatory measurement: MI, TI, derating and the 510(k) output table

Every transmit sequence ends in a number a reviewer checks: a derated intensity, a mechanical index, three thermal indices. This module rebuilds where each number comes from (the bioeffect it stands for, the measurement behind it, the derating and the depth where it is evaluated), what the current FDA guidance and IEC standards actually say, and how coded excitation, plane-wave ultrafast imaging, push pulses and contrast imaging move the numbers.

In 60 seconds
  1. Heat and mechanics follow different quantities: tissue heats over seconds, while cavitation (bubble activity) responds to one pulse. Conduction smooths absorbed power over \(\tau\approx a^2/4\kappa\) (a beam radius, κ thermal diffusivity), 1.9 s for a 1 mm beam, so heating follows time-average intensity. Cavitation follows one pulse's peak negative pressure. Hence two indices: thermal (TI) and mechanical (MI). §22.1
  2. Exposure quantities come from one waveform: every intensity on a safety report derives from the pressure waveform measured at a point. The pulse-intensity integral \(\mathrm{PII}=\int p^2/(\rho c)\,dt\) (ρ density, c sound speed) is energy per area per pulse; times the pulses per second reaching the point it gives time-average intensity. In plane-wave imaging every transmit reaches every point. §22.2
  3. Derating converts water to tissue: water barely attenuates, so measured output is reduced to estimate what tissue receives. The reduction is \(0.3\,fz\) dB (f in MHz, z depth in cm), applied as 20 log to pressure and 10 log to intensity: 4.5 dB at 3 MHz and 5 cm. The 0.3 sits below soft tissue to protect fluid paths. §22.3
  4. The mechanical index: MI rates cavitation risk as derated peak negative pressure (MPa) over √frequency (MHz), taken where derated energy per pulse peaks. The square root lets the limit MI 1.9 allow more pressure at higher frequency: 3.29 MPa derated at 3 MHz. Report the depth too. §22.4
  5. The thermal index is a power ratio, not a temperature: TI is output power over the power that would warm tissue 1 °C in a standard model; it matches the real maximum rise only within about a factor of 2. For soft tissue the reference is about 210/f mW (f in MHz): 70 mW at 3 MHz. §22.5
  6. Limits and their proof: the FDA output-display pathway (Track 3) caps derated time-average intensity at 720 mW/cm² with MI ≤ 1.9 and asks for an explanation above TI 6.0. Each index peaks at its own setting (MI at high voltage, short focus, low frequency), so each needs its own worst-case search: the model ranks settings, measurement reports. §22.6 §22.7
  7. New sequences move the binding limit: coded, plane-wave and push sequences keep the peak pressure but deliver far more energy per second, so intensity and thermal limits bind first. A 400 µs push carries about 600 times the energy per area of a 2-cycle pulse at the same pressure, so push and tracking are reported as one mode. §22.8
From the field
  • Run the worst-case output search per index, combined modes included, early in development. Each index peaks at a different setting, and combined modes such as B-mode plus colour plus pulsed Doppler add their contributions. This is where most FDA questions land: was the global maximum found? §22.7
  • Validate displayed MI and TI against measured output for every probe and mode. Display accuracy is part of the FDA submission. Expect TI to track the true maximum rise only within about a factor of 2, under-estimating by up to 2 in fetal exams through fluid; probe self-heating is tested separately. §22.5
  • Keep margin under the limits for measurement uncertainty and production spread. FDA estimates its Table 3 (Track 1) levels to be uncertain by ±30 % for intensity and ±15 % for MI. That is not headroom above a limit: a manufacturer whose uncertainty exceeds those margins must lower its levels by the excess, and unit-to-unit probe sensitivity spread comes on top. §22.6
  • Check fluid-path exams (full bladder, amniotic fluid) separately from derated numbers. The 0.3 dB/cm/MHz derating sits below tissue attenuation to protect such paths, yet through fluid at about 0.05 dB/cm/MHz the pressure in the body still exceeds the derated value. §22.3
  • Size the hydrophone to the focus and correct for spatial averaging. A hydrophone averages pressure over its face, so it under-reads a tight focus: a diameter of half the −6 dB beam width reads 0.74 dB low. A Gaussian-kernel correction restores the peak within 0.1 dB up to about three-quarters of the beam width. §22.7
  • Re-check time-average intensity and TI, not only MI, when adding coded excitation, plane waves or pushes. These sequences leave peak pressure alone but multiply energy per second, so intensity and thermal limits bind first: a chirp with time-bandwidth product 50 took spatial-peak time-average intensity from 27 to about 2000 mW/cm². §22.8
How to read the regulatory statements in this module

Numbers attributed to the FDA guidance (February 2023: Table 3, Sections 5.2.4–5.2.8) and the AIUM statements (2021; 2016, reapproved 2022) were checked against the current published text (October 2026). IEC clause-level details (formula constants, table layouts) are given only where a public source confirmed them; where the module relies on the ODS lineage or on a secondary source, it says so (the IEC 62359 definition of \(z_{\mathrm{MI}}\) as the depth of maximum attenuated PII is taken from published papers that cite the 2010 edition, and it matches the FDA glossary). Two items could not be confirmed from any public source and are flagged where they appear: the constant \(C_{\mathrm{TIB}}\) of the IEC 62359 \(\sqrt{P_{\alpha} I_{\mathrm{ta},\alpha}}\) form of TIB, and the surface-temperature limits of IEC 60601-2-37:2024. Always work from the edition of each standard your device declares conformity to. The calculators here illustrate the thermal and mechanical indices under simplified assumptions; they are not a substitute for IEC 62359 acoustic-field measurement and mode-dependent calculation, for IEC 60601-2-37 probe-temperature testing, or for a conformity assessment. TIS, TIB and TIC use different models, and a displayed TI does not include transducer self-heating.

22.1Two ways ultrasound acts on tissue: heat and mechanics

Heating. A wave of intensity I in a medium with intensity absorption coefficient \(\mu_a\) (twice the amplitude coefficient, in Np/m) deposits a power density \(q = \mu_a I\). Temperature follows the Pennes bioheat equation (22.1): conduction carries heat away over the beam radius, perfusion removes it over a perfusion length L of order 1 cm. Both act slowly compared with any pulse, so heating responds to the time-average intensity or power, never to the peak.

Thermal dose. CEM43 (Sapareto & Dewey 1984) converts any history to equivalent minutes at 43 °C: 128 min at 40 °C is 2 CEM43; 1 min at 45 °C is 4 CEM43.

What is safe thermally (AIUM, Statement on Biological Effects of Ultrasound in Vivo, 2021). A maximum in situ rise of 1.5 °C above 37 °C may be used clinically without reservation on thermal grounds. A fetal rise of 4 °C for 5 min or more can cause severe developmental defects; with commercial equipment this is unlikely at a fetal site if TI ≤ 2.5 and the dwell time is ≤ 4 min, and there is no time limit when TI stays below 0.7. Heating increases from B-mode to colour Doppler to M-mode to spectral Doppler and ARFI. Heating near bone is much larger than in soft tissue, so obstetric practice follows TIS to 10 weeks after the last menstrual period and TIB after.

Mechanics. Inertial cavitation needs a gas nucleus. For an optimally sized free gas nucleus in the diagnostic range, the threshold peak rarefactional pressure grows roughly as \(\sqrt{f}\) (Apfel & Holland 1991), which is why the mechanical index divides \(p_r\) by \(\sqrt{f}\). In tissue without gas bodies the AIUM finds no adverse non-thermal effects below MI 1.9, in vivo inertial cavitation thresholds at or above about 3.6 MPa for f ≥ 0.5 MHz, and lowest empirical thresholds of MI > 4 (blood-cell extravasation in mouse kidney). Gas bodies lower the thresholds sharply: pulmonary capillary haemorrhage in some mammals has a threshold MI near 0.4, intestinal effects near 1.4; the separate AIUM contrast-agent statement (reapproved 2021) reports bioeffects with gas-body contrast agents above MI 0.4. Radiation force and streaming are the third mechanism; ARFI and SWE use them on purpose (Section 22.8).

22.3Derating: 0.3 dB/cm/MHz, applied correctly

Fields are measured in water, which barely attenuates; tissue does. The derated ("attenuated", subscript α in IEC notation, ".3" in FDA notation) value multiplies the water value at depth z by the loss of a homogeneous model tissue with 0.3 dB cm\(^{-1}\) MHz\(^{-1}\) along the beam axis (22.11)–(22.12). The FDA guidance states the assumption in those words. The value is deliberately below typical soft tissue (about 0.5–1 dB/cm/MHz, Duck 1990) so that paths with long fluid segments, such as a full bladder or amniotic fluid, are not badly underestimated.

Pitfalls that change a derated number by tens of per cent

(1) Using ÷10 for pressure or ÷20 for intensity. (2) Derating the water spatial peak at its own depth instead of searching for the maximum of the derated profile. (3) Using the transducer nominal frequency instead of the measured acoustic working frequency. 22.4The mechanical index

IEC 62359 defines MI from the attenuated peak rarefactional pressure at a specific depth \(z_{\mathrm{MI}}\), the depth on the beam axis where the attenuated pulse-intensity integral is largest, and the acoustic working frequency \(f_{\mathrm{awf}}\) (from the hydrophone waveform at the position of spatial-peak temporal-peak pressure, as defined in IEC 62127-1). The FDA guidance (Appendix A glossary) uses the same depth in its formula. Its one-line prose definition ("the spatial-peak value of the peak rarefactional pressure, derated by 0.3 dB/cm-MHz at each point along the beam axis") reads like a maximum over depth, but the formula that follows fixes the point: \(\mathrm{MI} = p_{r.3}(z_{\mathrm{sp}})/\sqrt{f_c}\), where \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}\) is "the point on the beam axis where the pulse intensity integral (\(\mathrm{PII}_{.3}\)) is maximum" and \(f_c\) is the centre frequency (22.13). MI is therefore not the maximum of \(p_{r.3}(z)\) over depth; it is \(p_{r.3}\) read at the depth where the derated PII peaks.

MI indexes the likelihood of inertial cavitation of free gas nuclei and the scaling of gas-body effects, which is why it is displayed. 22.5The thermal indices: TIS, TIB, TIC

A thermal index is a power ratio: \(\mathrm{TI} = W/W_{\mathrm{deg}}\), the relevant (attenuated, bounded) acoustic power divided by the power that raises the model tissue by 1 °C. It is not a temperature, but the AIUM states that TI predicts the maximum temperature rise within a factor of 2, with TIS usually overestimating or close, and underestimating by up to 2 in fetal examinations through low-attenuation fluid. The tank. Degassed (and for high pressures deionized) water, absorbing walls, a 3-axis positioner with angular alignment, and a hydrophone with its preamplifier and calibration file. Alignment and the search for the beam axis and spatial peak come first, then axial scans, then lateral and elevation scans at the depths that matter (\(z_{\mathrm{MI}}\), \(z_s\), \(z_b\)).

22.8New sequences, new numbers: coding, ultrafast, push pulses, contrast and ULM

Coded excitation (Module 10). At a fixed peak pressure, MI is unchanged while PII, \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA}}\) and power scale with the energy ratio (the compression gain). A TB-50 chirp at MI 1.9 (5 MHz) and 200 pulses/s at a point goes from 27 mW/cm² for a 60 % Gaussian pulse of the same band to about 2000 mW/cm², so a coded mode is usually limited by \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}}\) or TI, not MI.

Plane-wave and diverging-wave ultrafast imaging. The unfocused transmit has a low peak pressure (low MI) but every transmit reaches every point, so \(I_{\mathrm{ta}} = \mathrm{PII}\times\mathrm{PRF}\). At 5 kHz this is 40–50 times the pulse rate a focused B-mode delivers to a point, and long acquisitions (Doppler ensembles of hundreds of frames, ULM acquisitions of seconds to minutes) turn that into TI and dwell-time questions.

Push pulses (ARFI, SWE). A push is a focused burst of hundreds of microseconds (hundreds to a few thousand cycles), often near the MI limit. Its PII is hundreds of times that of an imaging pulse, so a few pushes per second dominate \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}}\) and focal heating, and the tracking ensemble adds on top. The AIUM places ARFI with spectral Doppler at the top of the heating ranking and asks for 33 % shorter dwell times when bone is near the focus. Report the push and the tracking sequence as one combined mode, and evaluate heating at the push focus and at the transducer surface.

Contrast imaging and ULM. Low-MI contrast imaging is designed to keep bubbles oscillating without destroying them; high-MI flashes destroy them for replenishment studies. The pressure at the bubble is the in situ pressure at its depth, with the real attenuation of the path, not the derated pressure at \(z_{\mathrm{MI}}\): through a fluid path it is higher than the derated value, through liver it is lower. The AIUM contrast statement (reapproved 2021) reports bioeffects above MI 0.4 with gas-body agents and recommends the minimal MI and examination time; BMUS (2009) names MI 0.7 as the cavitation-risk threshold with contrast. ULM adds long plane-wave acquisitions at kHz rates: low MI per pulse and an \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA}}\) set by the PRF (a time average, independent of duration), while heating, and with it the TI dwell-time budget, accumulates over the whole acquisition.

  • A 400 µs push carries about 600 times the PII of a 2-cycle pulse at the same pressure; report push and tracking as one combined mode.
  • Bubbles see the in situ pressure with the real path loss, not the derated value; the AIUM reports bioeffects above MI 0.4 with gas-body contrast agents.

An expert keeps two ledgers. The mechanical ledger has one entry per pulse: the derated peak rarefactional pressure at the depth the standard specifies, divided by \(\sqrt{f}\) (22.13). 22.12Interview and qualifying-exam questions

I1Define MI and explain every factor in it. · screen

\(\mathrm{MI} = p_{r,\alpha}(z_{\mathrm{MI}})/(C_{\mathrm{MI}}\sqrt{f_{\mathrm{awf}}})\) with \(C_{\mathrm{MI}} = 1\ \mathrm{MPa\cdot MHz^{-1/2}}\) (22.13): the peak rarefactional pressure derated by 0.3 dB/cm/MHz, taken (IEC 62359 \(z_{\mathrm{MI}}\), FDA \(z_{\mathrm{sp}}\)) at the depth where the derated pulse-intensity integral peaks, divided by the root of the acoustic working frequency. Rarefaction because bubbles grow under tension; \(\sqrt{f}\) because the inertial-cavitation threshold of an optimally sized free nucleus rises roughly as \(\sqrt{f}\) (Apfel & Holland 1991). MI 1.9 at 3 MHz is 3.29 MPa derated.

Follow-up: does MI predict contrast-bubble destruction? Only loosely: shelled bubbles respond by resonance and shell properties, and the pressure at the bubble uses the real path attenuation.

I2Why is the derating 0.3 dB/cm/MHz when soft tissue is closer to 0.5–0.7? · screen

It is a deliberately low average so that exposure is not underestimated for paths with long fluid segments (full bladder, amniotic fluid) where attenuation is far below tissue values. The FDA states it as an assumed average attenuation along the beam axis. Pressure is derated by \(10^{-0.3fz/20}\) and intensity by \(10^{-0.3fz/10}\) (22.12): 4.5 dB at 3 MHz and 5 cm, × 0.596 and × 0.355.

Follow-up: when does derating still underestimate? Very low-loss paths and nonlinear water measurements at high drive, where energy already went into harmonics before derating.

I3State the Track 3 limits exactly, including the ophthalmic case. · regulatory

FDA guidance (February 2023): derated global maximum \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}} \le\) 720 mW/cm², and either \(\mathrm{MI} \le 1.9\) or derated \(I_{\mathrm{SPPA.3}} \le\) 190 W/cm². Ophthalmic: \(\mathrm{TI} = \max(\mathrm{TIS}_{\mathrm{as}}, \mathrm{TIC}) \le 1\), \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}} \le\) 50 mW/cm², \(\mathrm{MI} \le 0.23\). Any TI above 6.0 should be explained. Track 3 applies to devices conforming to the Output Display Standard in IEC 60601-2-37.

Follow-up: a short-pulse 3.5 MHz mode at MI 1.85 has \(I_{\mathrm{SPPA.3}}\) = 300 W/cm². Compliant? Yes, through the MI branch, provided \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}} \le 720\).

I4Track 1 or Track 3: what is the difference and when would you choose Track 1? · regulatory

Track 1 is for devices that do not conform to the IEC 60601-2-37 output display; their derated output is held to application-specific preamendments levels (\(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}}\) 720 peripheral vessel, 430 cardiac, 94 fetal imaging and other, 17 mW/cm² ophthalmic; \(I_{\mathrm{SPPA.3}}\) 190, or 28 W/cm² ophthalmic, or MI 1.9 / 0.23). Track 3 replaces these with global limits plus an on-screen MI/TI display. Track 1 suits simple, fixed-output devices (some Doppler-only or single-purpose devices) where a display adds cost but the low application limit is easy to meet.

Follow-up: a fetal-imaging device on Track 1 at 200 mW/cm²? Not acceptable: the fetal level is 94.

I5When must MI and TI be displayed, and what is reported if they never exceed 1? · regulatory

The display requirement comes from IEC 60601-2-37 (the Output Display Standard), which, as public secondary sources summarize it (the IEC text is not public), ties the obligation to whether the system can exceed an index value of 1.0. The FDA guidance follows the same threshold: if no system/transducer combination can exceed a TI of 1.0 or an MI of 1.0 in any operating mode, the global maximum values are submitted instead of per-mode reporting tables; tables are provided for combinations whose global maximum displayed MI or TI exceeds 1.0. Display accuracy against measurement is part of the submission.

Follow-up: which TI is displayed? The one appropriate to the application (TIS, TIB or TIC), with the user able to select it.

I6You add an SWE mode to a cleared system. Plan the acoustic-output work. · onsite

Treat push plus tracking as one combined mode. Measure the push waveform with a membrane hydrophone at the push focus and along the axis to find \(z_{\mathrm{MI}}\); derate; compute PII per push (a Gaussian-envelope push 400 µs long at −6 dB and 3 MPa is about 0.09 J/cm²; a rectangular 400 µs burst at the same pressure is 0.12 J/cm²), \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}}\) from push and tracking rates, \(I_{\mathrm{SPPA.3}}\), MI, and the TIs at the push and tracking powers. Search the global maximum over push voltage, duration, focal depth, push rate and region-of-interest size. Measure surface temperature under the worst sequence. Expect TI and \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}}\), not MI, to set the push repetition limit; check AIUM guidance on reduced dwell for ARFI near bone for labelling.

Follow-up: why can a compliant \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}}\) still be a heating concern? The average hides heating within a push and at the face; the TI and surface tests catch it.

I7Estimate \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}}\) for a 5 MHz plane-wave mode at 5 kHz PRF with \(p_{r.3}\) = 0.8 MPa and 2-cycle pulses. · onsite

\(\sigma_t = 2/(2.3548\cdot 5\ \mathrm{MHz}) = 0.170\ \mathrm{\mu s}\), \(\int \mathrm{env}^{2} = 0.301\ \mathrm{\mu s}\). \(\mathrm{PII} = \frac{p^{2}}{2\rho c}\cdot 0.301\ \mathrm{\mu s}\) \(= \frac{(0.8\ \mathrm{MPa})^{2}}{3\ \mathrm{MRayl}}\times 0.301\ \mathrm{\mu s}\) \(= 0.0642\ \mathrm{J/m^{2}}\) = 6.4 µJ/cm². Every transmit reaches the point: \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}}\) = 6.4 µJ/cm² × 5000/s = 32 mW/cm², \(\mathrm{MI} = 0.8/\sqrt{5} = 0.36\). A focused B-mode at the same pressure and 113 pulses/s would be 0.7 mW/cm².

Follow-up: a 60 s ULM acquisition at the same settings: \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA}}\) is the same, but TI and dwell time now govern; the AIUM tables apply to the displayed TI.

I8How do you choose a hydrophone for a 15 MHz linear array, and what corrections do you apply? · onsite

At 15 MHz and F/2 the focal \(W_{-6}\) is about 0.2 mm, so the effective diameter should be at most 50–100 µm (a spot-poled membrane, or a fibre-optic or small needle hydrophone with known effective radius), and the calibrated bandwidth should extend to several harmonics (≥ 60 MHz) because the focal waveform is nonlinear. Deconvolve with the complex sensitivity, apply the spatial-averaging correction of IEC 62127-1:2022, and include both in the uncertainty budget. A 200 µm hydrophone at \(W_{-6}\) = 0.2 mm would under-read p by 2.8 dB and intensity by about 48 %.

Follow-up: why do harmonics make it worse? Their beams are narrower, so averaging removes more of them and distorts \(p_c\) and \(p_r\) differently.

I9TIS, TIB or TIC: which applies when, and why does Doppler TIB exceed TIS? · screen

TIS for soft tissue (abdomen, vessels, early pregnancy), TIB when bone may lie at the beam's focus (obstetrics after 10 weeks LMP, Doppler near bone), TIC when bone is under the transducer (transcranial). Bone absorbs nearly all incident power in a thin layer, so its \(W_{\mathrm{deg}}\) scales with beam diameter (≈ 40 mW/cm × \(d\)), whereas soft tissue \(W_{\mathrm{deg}} = 210/f\) mW. A narrow Doppler focus therefore gives a small bone \(W_{\mathrm{deg}}\); in the PW preset TIB = 1.5 against TIS = 0.34.

Follow-up: why is TIB in scanned modes set equal to TIS? Sweeping the beam spreads the bone heating, and the surface soft-tissue case dominates.

I10What does the ±30 % / ±15 % statement in the FDA guidance mean for your uncertainty budget? · regulatory

The Track 1 preamendments levels are themselves estimated to be uncertain by ±30 % for intensities and ±15 % for MI. A manufacturer whose expanded measurement uncertainty is within those margins does not have to subtract it from the limits; if it exceeds them, the levels are reduced by the excess (an intensity uncertainty of 40 % reduces the level by 10 %). The protocol must describe Type A and Type B uncertainties: hydrophone calibration, spatial averaging, bandwidth, positioning, temperature and the power measurement.

Follow-up: which term usually dominates at high frequency? Hydrophone calibration and spatial averaging.

I11For CEUS and ULM, what pressure matters at the bubble, and why is the displayed MI a poor guide? · research

The bubble responds to the in situ pressure at its depth, with the real attenuation and the local beam, and to its resonance relative to \(f\); the displayed MI is the 0.3-derated pressure at one depth divided by \(\sqrt{f}\), a scaling derived for free gas nuclei. Through a fluid path (\(\alpha \approx\) 0.05) at 3.5 MHz and 8 cm the in situ pressure exceeds the derated value by 7.0 dB; through liver-like tissue (\(\alpha \approx\) 0.7) it is 11.2 dB lower. Researchers calibrate the in situ pressure (hydrophone plus a tissue model) and report it with the frequency and pulse length.

Follow-up: what MI threshold do safety statements give for contrast? The AIUM reports bioeffects above MI 0.4 with gas-body agents and recommends minimal MI and time; BMUS uses 0.7 as the cavitation-risk threshold.

22.14Self-check

Q1A 5 MHz probe measures \(p_r\) = 4.0 MPa in water at 3 cm. What are \(p_{r.3}\) and the MI if this is \(z_{\mathrm{MI}}\)?

\(A = 0.3\times 5\times 3 = 4.5\) dB, factor \(10^{-4.5/20} = 0.596\): \(p_{r.3}\) = 2.38 MPa, \(\mathrm{MI} = 2.38/\sqrt{5} = 1.07\).

Q2A water \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA}}\) of 600 mW/cm² is measured at 4 cm at 3.5 MHz. Is the derated value within Track 3?

\(A = 0.3\times 3.5\times 4 = 4.2\) dB; intensity factor \(10^{-0.42} = 0.380\): \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA.3}}\) = 228 mW/cm² ≤ 720, provided 4 cm is where the derated \(I_{\mathrm{ta}}\) peaks (search the profile, not one point).

Q3A scanned linear-array mode radiates 80 mW at 3.5 MHz across a 40 mm scan. What is TIS?

\(P_1 = 80\times(10/40) = 20\) mW through a 1 cm window; \(\mathrm{TIS} = 20\times 3.5/210 = 0.33\).

Q4Why does multiplying PII by the system PRF overstate \(I_{\mathrm{SPTA}}\) for focused B-mode but not for plane-wave imaging?

In focused scanning only the lines whose beams overlap the point deliver energy there (about \(0.75\,w_{-6}/\Delta\) per frame); in plane-wave imaging every transmit insonifies every point, so pulses per second at the point equal the PRF.

Q5A transcranial probe has a 2 cm² aperture and radiates 120 mW. What is TIC, and what does the AIUM non-obstetric table allow?

\(D_{\mathrm{eq}} = \sqrt{4\times 2/\pi} = 1.60\) cm; \(\mathrm{TIC} = 120/(40\times 1.60) = 1.88\). Non-obstetric (adult transcranial included): \(1.5 < \mathrm{TI} \le 2.0\), under 120 min dwell.

Q6A 0.5 mm hydrophone measures a round Gaussian focus with \(W_{-6}\) = 1.0 mm. How low are the pressure and intensity readings?

\(d/W_{-6} = 0.5\): \(\langle p\rangle/p_0 = 0.918\), −0.74 dB in pressure and the same −0.74 dB in intensity (\(0.918^{2} = 0.843\), 15.7 % low); MI reads 8 % low.

Q7Tissue sits at 41 °C for 30 min. What is the thermal dose in CEM43?

\(R\) = 0.25 below 43 °C: \(30\times 0.25^{2} = 1.9\) CEM43.
