Eigendecomposition & SVD

The singular value decomposition splits any matrix into orthogonal input directions, gains and orthogonal output directions. That one factorization gives you the best low-rank approximation, the stable pseudo-inverse, PCA, and the spatiotemporal clutter filter that is the first stage of most ULM pipelines. The same tool, applied to a distortion matrix, gives the aberration phase law.

You will be able to

  • Find eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and say when they are real and orthogonal (spectral theorem)
  • Read the SVD geometrically and relate \(\sigma_{i}\) to \(\operatorname{eig} (\mathbf{A}^{H}\mathbf{A})\), rank, \(\lVert \cdot \rVert_{2}, \lVert \cdot \rVert_{F}\) and κ
  • Build and bound the best rank-k approximation (Eckart–Young)
  • Build a Casorati matrix from an IQ ensemble, choose SVD cutoffs and explain why the filter survives tissue motion when a temporal high-pass fails

06.1Eigenvectors: the directions a matrix does not turn

A square matrix \(\mathbf{A}\) maps most vectors to a new direction. An eigenvector is a non-zero vector whose direction survives: the matrix only scales it, by the eigenvalue λ.

\[ \mathbf{A}\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v} \;\Leftrightarrow\; (\mathbf{A}-\lambda\mathbf{I})\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}\ \text{ with } \mathbf{v}\ne\mathbf{0} \;\Leftrightarrow\; \det(\mathbf{A}-\lambda\mathbf{I}) = 0 \]

Δ > 0: two real, distinct eigenvalues. Δ = 0: a repeated eigenvalue, possibly with only one eigenvector (defective). Δ < 0: a complex-conjugate pair and no real invariant direction (the matrix rotates every real vector). Always: \(\sum \lambda_{i} = \operatorname{tr} \mathbf{A}\), \(\prod \lambda_{i} = \det \mathbf{A}\).

If \(\mathbf{A}\) has n independent eigenvectors, stack them as columns of \(\mathbf{X}\): \(\mathbf{AX} = \mathbf{X}\Lambda\), so \(\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{X}\Lambda \mathbf{X}^{-1}\). In the eigenvector basis the matrix acts as n independent scalar gains, which is why powers and functions are easy: \(\mathbf{A}^{k} = \mathbf{X}\Lambda^{k}\mathbf{X}^{-1}\).

Spectral theorem: a Hermitian matrix is a weighted sum of orthogonal rank-1 projectors. Repeated eigenvalues still admit an orthonormal eigenbasis (choose one inside the eigenspace). If additionally \(\mathbf{A} \succeq 0\) (all \(\lambda_{i} \ge 0\)), as for \(\mathbf{A}^{H}\mathbf{A}\) or any covariance, the eigenvalues are variances along \(\mathbf{q}_{i}\).

For a non-symmetric matrix none of this is guaranteed: eigenvectors can be oblique (the angle between them can approach 0°, making \(\mathbf{X}\) ill-conditioned), eigenvalues can be complex, and the matrix can be defective (fewer than n eigenvectors). Shears and propagation operators with loss are typical examples. That fragility is why the SVD, which always exists with orthonormal bases on both sides, is the workhorse.

The eigenvectors of the channel covariance matrix \(\mathbf{R} = E[\mathbf{x} \mathbf{x}^{H}]\) (Hermitian, Module 07) split the aperture data into a coherent signal subspace and an incoherent noise subspace. Eigenspace-based beamformers project the minimum-variance weights onto the dominant eigenvectors, and the fraction \(\lambda_{1}/\sum \lambda_{i}\) is a coherence measure (the eigenvalue analogue of the coherence factor). Because \(\mathbf{R}\) is Hermitian, those eigenvalues are real powers and the eigenvectors are orthogonal aperture patterns.

Pitfall

Eigenvalues of a non-symmetric matrix can be hypersensitive. The shear [[1, ε],[0, 1]] has a double eigenvalue 1 for every ε, but [[1, ε],[δ, 1]] has \(\lambda = 1 \pm \sqrt{\varepsilon \delta}\): a perturbation \(\delta = 10^{-10}\) with ε = 1 moves λ by \(10^{-5}\). Singular values never do this: \(\lvert \sigma_{i}(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E}) - \sigma_{i}(\mathbf{A})\rvert \le \lVert \mathbf{E}\rVert_{2}\) (Weyl).

06.2The SVD: rotate, stretch, rotate

Every matrix \(\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{C}^{m\times n}\), square or not, full rank or not, factors as

\[ \mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\Sigma\mathbf{V}^{H} = \sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_i\,\mathbf{u}_i\mathbf{v}_i^{H}, \qquad \sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r > 0 \]

\(\mathbf{U} (m\times m)\) and \(\mathbf{V} (n\times n)\) are unitary; \(\Sigma\) is m×n diagonal with non-negative entries. \(r = \operatorname{rank} \mathbf{A}\). The thin form keeps only the first min(m,n) columns.

Existence in three lines. \(\mathbf{A}^{H}\mathbf{A}\) is Hermitian and positive semidefinite, so the spectral theorem gives \(\mathbf{A}^{H}\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{V}\Lambda \mathbf{V}^{H}\) with \(\lambda_{i} \ge 0\). Define \(\sigma_{i} = \sqrt{\lambda_{i}}\) and, for \(\sigma_{i} > 0, \mathbf{u}_{i} = \mathbf{Av}_{i}/\sigma_{i}\). These \(\mathbf{u}_{i}\) are orthonormal:

\[ \mathbf{u}_i^{H}\mathbf{u}_j = \frac{\mathbf{v}_i^{H}\mathbf{A}^{H}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{v}_j}{\sigma_i\sigma_j} = \frac{\lambda_j\,\mathbf{v}_i^{H}\mathbf{v}_j}{\sigma_i\sigma_j} = \delta_{ij} \]
(06.8)
\(\mathbf{u}_i,\ \mathbf{v}_i,\ \sigma_i\)
singular vectors and values, as in (06.7)
\(\mathbf{A}^{H}\mathbf{A}\)

\(\lambda_j\)

\((\cdot)^{H}\)

Complete the \(\mathbf{u}_{i}\) to an orthonormal basis of \(\mathbb{C}^{m}\). Then \(\mathbf{AV} = \mathbf{U}\Sigma\), i.e. \(\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{U}\Sigma \mathbf{V}^{H}\).

Geometrically, \(\mathbf{V}^{H}\) rotates the input so the right singular vectors \(\mathbf{v}_{i}\) land on the coordinate axes, \(\Sigma\) stretches axis i by \(\sigma_{i}\), and \(\mathbf{U}\) rotates the result into the output space. The unit sphere becomes an ellipsoid with semi-axes \(\sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\), and \(\mathbf{Av}_{i} = \sigma_{i}\mathbf{u}_{i}\) is the defining relation.

For a Hermitian positive semidefinite matrix the SVD and the eigendecomposition coincide (\(\sigma_{i} = \lambda_{i}, \mathbf{U} = \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{Q}\)). For a Hermitian indefinite matrix \(\sigma_{i} = \lvert \lambda_{i}\rvert\) and the sign moves into \(\mathbf{U}\). For a non-normal matrix the two are unrelated in general: in Demo 06.1, the shear preset has λ = 1, 1 but σ = 1.62, 0.62.

Intuition

Think of \(\mathbf{A}\) as a bank of n orthogonal “input beams” \(\mathbf{v}_{i}\), each received on its own orthogonal “output beam” \(\mathbf{u}_{i}\) with gain \(\sigma_{i}\) and no cross-talk. The SVD is the only basis pair that fully decouples the operator. For a transmit-receive matrix this is literally the DORT picture: \(\mathbf{v}_{1}\) is the transmit apodization-and-phase law that delivers the most energy back to the array, and \(\mathbf{u}_{1}\) is its received wavefront.

A rank-1 term is a separable pattern: spatial map × temporal course, or depth profile × lateral profile. Tissue moving rigidly is close to separable over a short ensemble; that single observation is the basis of the SVD clutter filter in 06.5. 06.4PCA, the pseudo-inverse and truncated SVD

PCA is the SVD of centered data

Model-based sound-speed tomography and aberration estimation solve \(\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{Ax}\) with an \(\mathbf{A}\) whose singular values decay fast (limited angular aperture, smooth slowness kernels). Truncated SVD and Tikhonov are the baseline regularizers; their spectral-filter form is how you reason about which slowness patterns a given aperture can and cannot recover.

Pitfall

“Rank” in floating point is a threshold decision. MATLAB's rank and pinv default to tol = \(\max (m,n)\cdot \varepsilon \cdot \sigma_{1} \approx 10^{-14}\sigma_{1}\), which suits exact data and is far too small for measured data. Set the threshold from the noise level, not from machine precision.

Why a high-pass fails under motion. Pitfall: choosing the cutoffs

The singular value curve rarely shows a clean knee. Failure modes: \((1) k_{\mathrm{lo}}\) too low leaves tissue residue that looks like flow, worst near bright, moving interfaces; \((2) k_{\mathrm{lo}}\) too high removes slow flow and large vessels, whose spatial singular vectors are themselves coherent; (3) tissue motion that is non-rigid (cardiac, breathing over a long ensemble) spreads tissue over many ranks so no single \(k_{\mathrm{lo}}\) works, which is why practice uses short ensembles, blockwise (local) SVD, or motion correction first; \((4) k_{\mathrm{hi}}\) too high adds noise, which also inflates PD at depth where noise dominates. Data-driven rules exist: the knee of the curve, the temporal singular vectors' Doppler content (as in this demo), or the similarity of the spatial singular vectors (Baranger et al. 2018), which groups ranks whose \(\lvert \mathbf{u}_{k}\rvert\) maps look alike.

06.6Where this goes next

  • Eigenspace beamforming (Module 25). The minimum-variance weights \(\mathbf{w}\) \(= \mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{a}/(\mathbf{a}^{H}\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{a})\) are projected onto the dominant eigenvectors of the sample covariance \(\mathbf{R}\) (Asl & Mahloojifar 2010), which suppresses the noise-subspace contribution that makes plain MV speckle grainy. The spectral theorem of 06.1 is the entire mechanism.
  • Reflection and distortion matrices (aberration correction). Record the full transmit–receive response \(\mathbf{R}\), either element-by-element or in a plane-wave basis. Focusing it in a focused basis and then expressing each focal spot's residual wavefront relative to the ideal (homogeneous-medium) wavefront gives the distortion matrix \(\mathbf{D}\). Over an isoplanatic patch every focal point sees the same aberration, so the rows of \(\mathbf{D}\) are the same phase law times different speckle: \(\mathbf{D}\) is approximately rank 1, and its first singular vector is the aberration phase law to apply in transmit and receive (Lambert, Cobus, Frappart, Fink & Aubry 2020). Speckle averaging across the patch is what makes the estimate robust; patch size against isoplanatic angle is the key trade-off.
  • DORT. The SVD of the inter-element response matrix of a few point-like scatterers gives one significant σ per scatterer, and \(\mathbf{v}_{i}\) is the time-reversal focusing law onto scatterer i. It is the ancestor of both points above.
  • ULM. SVD clutter filtering (§06.5) → localization of bubble PSFs (Module 11 delay/peak estimation, Batch 2 estimation theory and CRLB) → tracking → rendering.
  • Sound-speed estimation. Inversion operators are ill-conditioned; the Picard plot and truncated SVD of 06.4 are how you read their resolving power.

The clutter filter \(\mathbf{SV}_{b}\mathbf{V}_{b}^{H}\) and PD are therefore well defined; an individual temporal vector's phase is not.

06.10Interview and qualifying-exam questions

I1What is the relation between PCA, the eigendecomposition of a covariance and the SVD? Which one do you compute, and why? · screen

For centered data \(\mathbf{X} (N\times p), \mathbf{X} = \mathbf{U}\Sigma \mathbf{V}^{H}\) gives \(\mathbf{C}\) \(= \mathbf{X}^{H}\mathbf{X}/(N-1)\) \(= \mathbf{V}[\Sigma^{2}/(N-1)]\mathbf{V}^{H}\): principal axes = right singular vectors, variances = \(\sigma^{2}/(N-1)\), scores = \(\mathbf{U}\Sigma\). Compute the SVD of \(\mathbf{X}\) when accuracy matters, because forming \(\mathbf{C}\) squares κ; use the eigendecomposition of the small Gram/covariance matrix when speed matters and the needed components are well above \(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\cdot \sigma_{1}\). Follow-up: "Does centering matter for a Casorati matrix?" Yes: removing each pixel's temporal mean is a degree-0 regression wall filter, so \(\sigma_{1}\) then describes tissue motion rather than static brightness.

I2Walk me through an SVD clutter filter, and explain why it beats a high-pass filter when tissue moves. · screen

Reshape the n frames into the Casorati matrix \(\mathbf{S}\) (pixels × frames), take its SVD (in practice the eigendecomposition of \(\mathbf{S}^{H}\mathbf{S}\)), keep the band (06.15) \([k_{\mathrm{lo}}, k_{\mathrm{hi}}]\): \(\mathbf{S}_{f} = \mathbf{SV}_{b}\mathbf{V}_{b}^{H}\), and PD = \((1/n)\sum_{\mathrm{band}}\sigma_{k}^{2}\lvert u_{k}\rvert^{2}\). A high-pass is a projection onto a fixed frequency band, so tissue at \(f_{D} = 2v/\lambda\) (52 Hz at 8 mm/s, 5 MHz) that overlaps flow Doppler cannot be removed without removing flow. The SVD projection is data-derived: coherent tissue occupies its own temporal vectors even at the same \(f_{D}\). Numbers from the MATLAB lab: at 8 mm/s, SVD 15.2 dB vessel contrast vs −0.4 dB for the best IIR; at rest, 24.7 vs 23.1 dB. Follow-up: "When does the SVD lose?" Large vessels (coherent, climb into the tissue ranks), non-rigid or out-of-plane motion (tissue spreads into the blood ranks), noise-dominated depth.

I3How do you choose the SVD thresholds automatically? · onsite

\(k_{\mathrm{hi}}\): from the noise model. Estimate \(\sigma_{n}\) (no-transmit frames, or match the median singular value to the Marchenko–Pastur median) and keep \(\sigma_{k} > \sigma_{n}(\sqrt{m}+ \sqrt{n})\), or use the Gavish–Donoho threshold \((4/\sqrt{3})\sqrt{n}\sigma_{n}\) for a square matrix. \(k_{\mathrm{lo}}\): from what distinguishes tissue: (1) the spatial-similarity matrix of \(\lvert \mathbf{u}_{k}\rvert\) (Baranger et al. 2018), where tissue ranks form a correlated block; (2) the Doppler content of the temporal vectors (mean or RMS frequency above a tissue threshold); (3) the knee of \(\sigma_{k}\) in dB; (4) energy-based rules (cut when the cumulative tissue energy reaches a fraction). Per block if the field is non-stationary. The best \(k_{\mathrm{lo}}\) moves with tissue motion (2 → 15 for 0 → 24 mm/s in the MATLAB sweep), which is why it must be adaptive. Follow-up: "How do you validate a threshold rule without ground truth?" Phantoms with known flow, in vivo reproducibility, residual tissue at bright moving interfaces, slow-flow preservation in known vessels, and for ULM the localization count and velocity distribution vs \(k_{\mathrm{lo}}\).

I4What happens to SVD filtering under large tissue motion, and what do you do about it? · onsite

Rank-1 requires separability. A uniform phase rotation stays rank 1, but speckle that slides across the PSF needs more ranks, roughly one extra per \(\sigma_{z}\) of displacement over the ensemble (0.1 mm → 3 ranks, 1 mm → 13 ranks to −50 dB in the module's simulation). Tissue then spreads into the ranks where blood lives, the optimal \(k_{\mathrm{lo}}\) rises and contrast drops (16.5 → 6.4 dB from 4 to 24 mm/s in the MATLAB sweep). Remedies: shorter ensembles or sliding windows (less displacement per window), motion estimation and registration before the SVD, block-wise SVD with local cutoffs, rejecting frames with large motion, and higher-order methods that model motion explicitly. Follow-up: "What does a shorter ensemble cost?" Doppler resolution PRF/N, fewer blood ranks, higher PD variance.

I5What is the computational cost of an SVD clutter filter, and how would you make it real-time? · onsite

Thin SVD of an M×N Casorati matrix (M ≫ N) is \(O(MN^{2})\). The fast route: \(G = \mathbf{S}^{H}\mathbf{S}\) (\(MN^{2}\), a GEMM that GPUs do well), eig of \(G (N^{3})\), then \(\mathbf{SV}_{b}\) (MNb). For 128×128 pixels and 300 frames: \(1.5\times 10^{9}\) complex MACs for \(G, 2.7\times 10^{7}\) for the eig. Further: compute only the leading \(k_{\mathrm{hi}}\) vectors (randomized SVD or subspace iteration, O(MNk)), update across overlapping windows instead of recomputing, use blocks in parallel. Follow-up: "Any catch with single precision?" Yes: forming G squares κ, so \(\sigma_{k}\) below about \(\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{single}}}\cdot \sigma_{1}\) (−69 dB) are corrupted; measured on the simulated ensemble the first > 1 % error appears at −66.8 dB, right at the blood/noise boundary. Either keep G in double, or run the SVD on \(\mathbf{S}\) directly.

I6Why are singular vectors unique only up to sign or phase, and when does that bite you? · onsite

\(\sigma \mathbf{uv}^{H}\) \(= \sigma (\mathbf{u}e^{j\varphi})(\mathbf{v}e^{j\varphi})^{H}\), so any common phase is valid, and for a repeated σ any unitary rotation within the singular subspace is valid. Only the projectors onto groups of singular vectors separated by a gap are unique, with perturbation sensitivity of order \(\lVert \mathbf{E}\rVert /\text{gap}\). It bites when you average or track vectors across blocks, windows or acquisitions (align phases first, or compare \(\lvert u_{k}\rvert\) or projectors), when you compare MATLAB with another library (signs differ), and inside the noise bulk where gaps are tiny. The filter \(\mathbf{SV}_{b}\mathbf{V}_{b}^{H}\) and PD are invariant. Follow-up: "How do you align two sets of singular vectors?" Orthogonal Procrustes: the SVD of \(\mathbf{V}_{1}^{H}\mathbf{V}_{2}\) gives the best unitary alignment.

I7Prove that the truncated SVD is the best rank-k approximation in the spectral norm. Does the same hold in Frobenius norm? · research

\(\lVert \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{A}_{k}\rVert_{2} = \sigma_{k+1}\) by unitary invariance (06.9). For any \(\mathbf{B}\) with rank ≤ k, \(\operatorname{null} \mathbf{B}\) has dimension ≥ n − k and therefore intersects the (k+1)-dimensional \(\operatorname{span} (\mathbf{v}_{1}, \dots, \mathbf{v}_{k+1})\) in a unit vector \(\mathbf{w}\). Then \(\lVert (\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B})\mathbf{w}\rVert\) \(= \lVert \mathbf{Aw}\rVert \ge \sigma_{k+1}\). Frobenius: Weyl's inequality \(\sigma_{i+j-1}(\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{Y}) \le \sigma_{i}(\mathbf{X}) + \sigma_{j}(\mathbf{Y})\) with \(\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\), \(\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{B}\), \(j = k+1\) gives \(\sigma_{k+i}(\mathbf{A}) \le \sigma_{i}(\mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B})\), so \(\lVert \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{B}\rVert_{F}^{2} \ge \sum_{i>k}\sigma_{i}^{2}\). It holds for every unitarily invariant norm (Mirsky). Follow-up: "Is the minimizer unique?" In Frobenius norm yes if \(\sigma_{k} > \sigma_{k+1}\); in the spectral norm no, many matrices achieve \(\sigma_{k+1}\).

I8What does the dominant eigenvector of the channel (spatial) covariance matrix mean physically? Connect it to DORT and aberration correction. · research

\(\mathbf{R} = E[\mathbf{xx}^{H}]\) across the aperture. For a single dominant scatterer \(\mathbf{x} = a\mathbf{g}\) + noise, so \(\mathbf{R}\) \(= E\lvert a\rvert^{2}\mathbf{gg}^{H} + \sigma_{n}^{2}\mathbf{I}\); the dominant eigenvector is the Green's function \(\mathbf{g}\) (including any aberration), and its phase conjugate is the transmit law that refocuses on the scatterer. \(\lambda_{1}/\sum \lambda_{i}\) measures coherence (a coherence-factor analogue). DORT applies the same idea to the inter-element response matrix \(\mathbf{K}\): one significant singular value per resolved point scatterer, each singular vector a selective focusing law (Prada & Fink 1994); iterative time reversal is power iteration on \(\mathbf{K}^{H}\mathbf{K}\). In speckle there is no single scatterer, so the distortion-matrix method averages over an isoplanatic patch to recover a rank-1 structure whose first singular vector is the aberration law. Follow-up: "What breaks it?" Scatterers within one resolution cell (vectors mix), patches larger than the isoplanatic area (rank grows), low SNR (\(\sigma_{1}\) near the noise bulk).

I9A static tissue ensemble is rank 1. Is tissue moving with a uniform axial Doppler shift also rank 1? What actually raises the rank? · research

If the scene only changes phase, \(\mathbf{S}\) \(= \mathbf{s}_{0}[e^{j2\pi f_{D}t_{1}} \dots e^{j2\pi f_{D}t_{n}}]\) is an outer product: rank 1 at any \(f_{D}\), which a high-pass filter cannot remove if \(f_{D}\) is in the passband but the SVD removes in one term. A real displacement also moves the envelope relative to the pixel grid; each pixel then sees a different, time-varying speckle amplitude, and the pattern is no longer separable. The number of ranks grows with displacement over the PSF size: about 3 ranks for \(1\sigma_{z}\), 6 for \(3\sigma_{z}\), 13 for \(10\sigma_{z}\) at a −50 dB residual. Follow-up: "So how would you make moving tissue low rank again?" Register the frames (undo the displacement) before the SVD, or work in blocks small enough that motion is locally a pure translation over a short window.

I10Where is the noise floor in a singular-value plot, and can a component below that floor still be detected? · research

For i.i.d. noise of standard deviation σ per entry, the noise singular values fill the Marchenko–Pastur bulk \([\sigma (\sqrt{m}- \sqrt{n}), \sigma (\sqrt{m}+ \sqrt{n})]\); for 1600 pixels × 80 frames that is 31.1σ to 48.9σ, only 4.0 dB wide (3.8 dB measured), so the tail of a Casorati spectrum looks like a gently sloping plateau. A rank-1 component of strength θ separates from the bulk only above \(\theta_{c} = \sigma (mn)^{1/4} = 18.9\sigma\) (the BBP transition); just above it \(\sigma_{1}\) barely clears the edge (\(\theta = 1.2\theta_{c}\) gives \(\sigma_{1} = 49.4\sigma\) vs edge 48.9σ) and its singular vectors are only partly aligned with the truth (\(\lvert \langle \mathbf{v}_{1}, \mathbf{v}\rangle \rvert^{2} = 0.45\)). Below \(\theta_{c}\) it is invisible to any SVD threshold. Follow-up: "Why are the temporal vectors better estimated than the spatial ones?" The overlap formulas weight by the aspect ratio: with \(m \gg n\), \(\lvert \langle \mathbf{v}_{1}\), \(\mathbf{v}\rangle \rvert^{2} = 0.89\) but \(\lvert \langle \mathbf{u}_{1}, \mathbf{u}\rangle \rvert^{2} = 0.44\) at \(\theta = 2\theta_{c}\): the n-dimensional temporal vector is estimated from m ≫ n pixels of evidence, while the m-dimensional spatial vector has only n frames.

I11Your ensemble is 128 frames at 1 kHz with a 5 MHz probe. What Doppler and velocity resolution do the temporal singular vectors have, and what would doubling the ensemble change? · screen

Δf = PRF/N = 7.8 Hz, i.e. (λ/2)Δf = 0.154 mm × 7.8 Hz = 1.20 mm/s axial; Nyquist ±500 Hz = ±77 mm/s. Doubling to 256 frames halves Δf to 3.9 Hz (0.60 mm/s), adds blood ranks and lowers PD variance, but doubles the time over which tissue moves (more tissue ranks, possibly non-stationary motion), quadruples the Gram cost \(MN^{2}\) and halves the PD frame rate if windows do not overlap. Follow-up: "Can you measure a 0.5 mm/s flow with 1.2 mm/s resolution?" Its mean frequency can be estimated below Δf at high SNR; resolving it from tissue at a similar Doppler needs the spatial separation the SVD provides.

Q2Why are the eigenvalues of a channel covariance matrix real and non-negative, and its eigenvectors orthogonal? Would that still hold for the matrix \(\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2\\ 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}\)?

\(\mathbf{R} = E[\mathbf{xx}^{H}]\) is Hermitian, so λ is real and eigenvectors for distinct λ are orthogonal (06.1 derivations). It is also PSD: \(\mathbf{w}^{H}\mathbf{Rw}\) \(= E\lvert \mathbf{w}^{H}\mathbf{x}\rvert^{2} \ge 0\), the output power of beamformer \(\mathbf{w}\). For [[1, 2],[0, 3]] the eigenvalues are 1 and 3 (triangular), eigenvectors (1, 0) and (1, 1): angle 45°, not orthogonal. Its singular values are \(\sqrt{7 \pm \sqrt{40}} = 3.65\) and 0.82, different from |λ|.

Q3A Casorati matrix has 128×128 pixels and 300 frames. What are the sizes of \(\mathbf{U}, \Sigma, \mathbf{V}\) in the thin SVD, and what is the cheapest way to compute the filter?

m = 16384, n = 300. Thin SVD: \(\mathbf{U} 16384\times 300\), \(\Sigma 300\times 300\), \(\mathbf{V} 300\times 300\). Cheapest: form the 300×300 Gram matrix \(\mathbf{S}^{H}\mathbf{S}\) (cost \(mn^{2} \approx 1.5\times 10^{9}\) complex multiply-adds), eigendecompose it (\(n^{3} \approx 2.7\times 10^{7}\)), then \(\mathbf{S}_{f} = (\mathbf{S} \mathbf{V}_{b})\mathbf{V}_{b}^{H}\) with cost 2mn·b. You never need the full \(\mathbf{U}\). The Gram route squares κ, acceptable because the band of interest is well above \(\varepsilon \cdot \sigma_{1}^{2}\).

Q4Tissue moves at 10 mm/s axially, \(f_{0} = 6\) MHz, PRF = 1 kHz. What Doppler frequency does it produce, and why does a 50 Hz high-pass fail? Why can the SVD still separate it from blood at the same Doppler?

\(\lambda = 1540/6\times 10^{6} = 0.257\) mm, \(f_{D} = 2v/\lambda = 2\times 10/0.257 = 78\) Hz. The tissue lies above the 50 Hz cutoff and passes with 40–60 dB of excess energy, swamping blood. Raising the cutoff above 78 Hz removes any blood with axial velocity below 10 mm/s. The SVD separates by spatial coherence and energy: the moving tissue is one large, nearly rigid pattern (a few rank-1 terms with high σ), while blood at the same \(f_{D}\) occupies small, independent regions and lower σ. The time courses can overlap in frequency yet live in orthogonal temporal singular vectors because the spatial maps differ.

Q5With the SVD cached, what does changing \(k_{\mathrm{lo}}\) cost, and why do the cross terms vanish in PD = \((1/n)\sum_{t}\lvert S_{f}\rvert^{2}\)?

\(\mathrm{PD}(p) = (1/n)\lVert \mathbf{s}_{f}(p)\rVert^{2}\) where the row \(\mathbf{s}_{f}(p)\) \(= \sum_{k\in \mathrm{band}}\sigma_{k}u_{k}(p)\mathbf{v}_{k}^{H}\). Since \(\mathbf{v}_{k}^{H}\mathbf{v}_{l} = \delta_{kl}\), \(\lVert \mathbf{s}_{f}(p)\rVert^{2}\) \(= \sum_{k}\sigma_{k}^{2}\lvert u_{k}(p)\rvert^{2}\). This is (06.16). With prefix sums over k it is O(m) per change; directly it is O(m·b). No SVD and no per-frame work.

Q6In the truncated-SVD deconvolution, the noise has standard deviation s per sample and \(\sigma_{i} = 10^{-i/4}\). The noise-free data coefficients are \(\lvert \mathbf{u}_{i}^{H}\mathbf{b}_{0}\rvert \approx 0.5\sigma_{i}\) and \(s = 10^{-3}\). Where should you truncate?

The data coefficient is \(\lvert \mathbf{u}_{i}^{H}\mathbf{b}\rvert \approx \sigma_{i}\cdot 0.5\) + noise of size s (the projection of white noise on a unit vector keeps standard deviation s). Signal exceeds noise while \(0.5\cdot 10^{-i/4} > 10^{-3}\), i.e. \(i < 4\cdot \log_{10}500 = 10.8\). Truncate at k ≈ 10. Beyond that each term adds noise amplified by \(1/\sigma_{i}\) (already \(10^{3}\cdot s = 1\) per term at i = 12) and almost no signal.

Q7Why is the distortion matrix over an isoplanatic patch approximately rank 1, and what limits the quality of its first singular vector?

Within an isoplanatic patch every focal point sees the same aberration transmittance H(u) across the aperture. Each row of \(\mathbf{D}\) (one focal point) is then H(u) multiplied by the point's speckle-dependent “virtual guide star” wavefront, which after the focusing operation is a broad, random-phase spot. The rows share the common factor H, so \(\mathbf{D} \approx (\text{speckle coefficients})\cdot H^{T}\) plus fluctuations: rank 1 with \(\mathbf{v}_{1} \approx H\). Quality is limited by the number of independent speckle realizations (more focal points → better averaging, \(\sigma_{1}\) grows faster than the fluctuation \(\sigma_{2}\)), by the patch exceeding the isoplanatic area (the aberration then varies across rows and rank grows), by the size of the initial focal spot, and by noise.

Q8The 2×2 rotation-by-θ matrix has no real eigenvectors. What are its eigenvalues and singular values, and what does that say about using eigenvalues to measure “gain”?

\(\lambda = \cos \theta \pm j \sin \theta = e^{\pm j\theta}\) with complex eigenvectors \((1, \mp j)/\sqrt{2}\). Its singular values are both 1: rotation preserves length (orthogonal matrix, \(\mathbf{A}^{T}\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{I}\)). For a normal matrix \(\lvert \lambda_{i}\rvert = \sigma_{i}\); here both equal 1. For non-normal matrices \(\lvert \lambda \rvert_{\max}\) can be far below \(\sigma_{1}\) (shear: \(\lvert \lambda \rvert = 1, \sigma_{1} = 1.62\)), so eigenvalues describe asymptotic growth of \(\mathbf{A}^{k}\), while \(\sigma_{1}\) is the worst-case one-step gain.
